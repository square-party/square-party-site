# PA-3 Campaign

Paul is running as a write-in candidate for the U.S. House of Representatives in Pennsylvania's 3rd Congressional District. The campaign is being conducted on Square Party terms — which is to say, as much about the framework as about the office.

## Two tracks

The campaign runs on two parallel tracks that share infrastructure but serve different purposes.

**The voter track** is for people who want to pledge a write-in vote. One question, one email, one click. *(A pledge widget will live here once the campaign launches more publicly.)*

**The content track** is the deeper work — applying the [People's Assembly](/square-party/assembly/) framework to PA-3 specifically, mapping where formal representation falls short of lived experience, and inviting contributors to push back on the analysis. That's the **[empower project](/paul/campaign/empower/)**.

The two tracks feed each other but don't block each other. Pledging a vote doesn't require reading the analysis. Reading the analysis doesn't require pledging a vote.

## Why a write-in

Pennsylvania's ballot-access rules for new parties and independents are demanding. The write-in path keeps the focus on the actual work — the framework, the analysis, the engagement — rather than on the procedural challenge of getting on a ballot. It's also an honest reflection of where this stands: a project that gets to the ballot through write-in support is a project that has earned that support on its substance, not on its access.

The third-party precedent here is Roosevelt's 1912 Bull Moose run — the most successful third-party presidential campaign in U.S. history, which still lost. The honest framing is that a write-in House campaign in PA-3 isn't trying to be the exception that wins; it's trying to be a campaign that *changes the conversation* about what representation could look like, and uses the cycle to put the framework in front of people who might pick it up after.
