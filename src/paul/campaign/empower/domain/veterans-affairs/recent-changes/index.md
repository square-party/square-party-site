# Recent Changes — Veterans Affairs

<p class="domain-lead">Eleven substantive Material Changes plus foundational historical context have reshaped the Veterans Affairs architecture across 2024-2026. The verification cycle's aggregate finding — federal statutes themselves substantively pro-veteran and stable (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pact-act">PACT Act</a> expansion; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#mission-act">MISSION Act</a> community care; pro-claimant adjudication standards at § 5107 / § 5103A; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hud-vash">HUD-VASH</a> joint architecture; Vets First Contracting at § 8127 with the Kingdomware mandatory rule of two; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ama">AMA</a> three-lane appeals architecture) <em>and</em> a pattern of operational-implementation conditions that limit substantive delivery (PACT Act adjudication-guidance gaps on nonpresumptive conditions; community-care scheduling time frames not yet established eight years after MISSION Act enactment; HUD-VASH voucher-to-housing-placement conversion gap in Philadelphia's constrained rental market; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#bva">BVA</a> throughput timelines for an aging Vietnam-era veteran population) — is composed of these events. <strong>The most consequential operational-trajectory change is MC-01 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#vba">VBA</a> backlog trajectory</strong> — peaked at approximately 417,855 in January 2024 in the wake of PACT Act volume; reduced to ~100,115 by January 2026 (76.0% total reduction); processing time approximately 75.7 days as of March 2026; the largest sustained backlog reduction in VBA history (G24-SD2-02 trajectory documented; substantive throughput improvement). <strong>The most consequential local-trajectory change is MC-05 Philadelphia veteran homelessness reversal</strong> — 284 veterans experienced homelessness in 2025, a 20% increase from 2024, breaking the national 14-year reduction trajectory in PA-3's local context (Project HOME February 2026 citing 2025 Point-in-Time count; MC42 Both/And presentation strengthened on the gap side). <strong>The most consequential cross-cutting administrative change is MC-04 GAO MISSION Act trajectory</strong> — as of February 2026 only 9 of 27 GAO recommendations implemented; 17 remaining unimplemented eight years after MISSION Act enactment (GAO-26-108943, March 4, 2026; community-care scheduling time frames among unimplemented). <strong>The most consequential statutory addition is MC-11 Dole Act</strong> (Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act; P.L. 118-210, 2025) — expanded community care, eliminated a secondary-physician-review step in "best medical interest" determinations effective May 2025, expanded caregiver support program access, and added new community-care mandates on top of unimplemented MISSION Act recommendations. <strong>The most consequential adjudication-accuracy change is MC-07 PACT Act corrective action</strong> — <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#va">VA</a> committed to implement all April 2025 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#oig">OIG</a> recommendations by July 31, 2025; September 2025 OIG found ongoing accuracy problems on nonpresumptive conditions; OIG follow-up initiated December 29, 2025; corrective-action completion unconfirmed as of verification date (G24-SD2-01 remains an active gap). <strong>The most consequential rate change is MC-03 2026 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cola">COLA</a></strong> — 2.8% effective December 1, 2025; 100% disability rate $3,938.58/month (no dependents). <strong>The most consequential throughput-measurement change is MC-02 BVA FY2025 final data</strong> — Direct Review approximately 506 days (improving from ~640 days mid-2024; BVA target 365 days); Evidence Submission approximately 713 days; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hlr">HLR</a> approximately 60.7 days (February 2026). <strong>The most consequential federal-spending change is MC-08 OBBBA</strong> (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025) — no direct VHA restructuring; indirect Medicaid work-requirement effects on approximately 1.75 million veterans (100%-disabled exempted) and SNAP work-requirement changes affecting approximately 1.4 million veterans; OBBBA contributed to the 2025 government shutdown (43-day FY2026 spending deadlock); continuation appropriations maintained VA operations. <strong>The most consequential employment-data change is MC-09 VETS-4212</strong> — reporting threshold raised $150,000 → $200,000 (all PA-3 anchor institutions remain subject); data.dol.gov public portal launched February 18, 2026; VEVRAA enforcement resumed July 2, 2025 (D10 SD5 cross-reference confirmed). <strong>The most consequential housing-allocation history change is MC-10 HUD-VASH voucher allocation</strong> — under 250 vouchers allocated to Philadelphia Housing Authority 2017-2024 versus 687 from 2008-2016 (plus 100 additional vouchers March 2024); Philadelphia HUD-VASH inventory approximately 350+; allocation gap quantitatively documented as one driver of MC-05's local trajectory reversal. <strong>The most consequential IT-infrastructure change is MC-06 Oracle Cerner <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ehr">EHR</a></strong> — three-year "reset" pause ended; rollout resumed April 2026 using a geographic-wave approach; only 6-7 VA facilities nationwide deployed as of April 2026; CMCVAMC remains on VistA with deployment timeline not yet confirmed.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Foundational federal architecture — VA elevated to Cabinet-level Department</h2>
</header>
<p>The Department of Veterans Affairs Act of 1988 (P.L. 100-527; 38 U.S.C. § 301) elevated the Veterans Administration to Cabinet-level status effective March 15, 1989, consolidating VHA (healthcare), VBA (benefits), and NCA (memorial affairs) under unified Secretary-level oversight at 810 Vermont Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20420. Title 38 of the U.S. Code organizes the substantive program architecture: Chapter 11 (compensation), Chapter 15 (pension), Chapter 17 (hospital and outpatient care), Chapter 31 (Veteran Readiness &amp; Employment), Chapter 33 (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#post-9-11-gi-bill">Post-9/11 GI Bill</a>), Chapter 37 (home loan guaranty), Chapter 71 (BVA), Chapter 72 (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cavc">CAVC</a>). <strong>Statutory layer composite: HIGH stability — the foundational Title 38 architecture has been incrementally expanded rather than eroded; 2025-2026 changes operate within rather than against the statutory framework.</strong></p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: all seven sub-domains. Sources: P.L. 100-527 at <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/STATUTE-102/STATUTE-102-Pg2635">102 Stat. 2635</a>; <a href="https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38">38 U.S.C.</a>.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Post-9/11 GI Bill — Chapter 33 substantive education entitlement architecture</h2>
</header>
<p>The Post-9/11 Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2008 (Pub. L. 110-252; 38 U.S.C. § 3301 et seq.) created Chapter 33 — full in-state public-college tuition coverage plus monthly housing allowance (MHA) at the local E-5-with-dependents BAH rate plus annual books-and-supplies stipend. The Yellow Ribbon Program (38 U.S.C. § 3317) supplements Chapter 33 for private institutions and out-of-state tuition above the federal cap. Transferability of unused entitlement to dependents was added; the program created the structural foundation for the PA-3 anchor-institution veteran-student architecture at Penn, Drexel, Temple, and Thomas Jefferson University.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/education-benefits/">VA Education Benefits</a> (principal anchor at SD3). Sources: Pub. L. 110-252 at <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/110th-congress/house-bill/2642">H.R. 2642</a>; 38 U.S.C. § 3301 et seq.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Appeals Modernization Act — three-lane appeals architecture replaces legacy system</h2>
</header>
<p>The Veterans Appeals Improvement and Modernization Act of 2017 (AMA; Pub. L. 115-55) restructured the VA appeals system into three discrete review lanes: Higher-Level Review (HLR) at the agency of original jurisdiction; Supplemental Claim (with new and relevant evidence); and BVA appeal (Direct Review, Evidence Submission, or Hearing docket). The AMA replaced the legacy traditional / DRO / NOD pathway that had produced multi-year claim development cycles. Pro-claimant standards at 38 U.S.C. § 5107 (benefit of the doubt) and § 5103A (duty to assist) operate across all three lanes and through CAVC review at 38 U.S.C. Chapter 72. <strong>The AMA's three-lane design is the architecture against which MC-02's FY2025 final processing times are measured.</strong></p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/appeals/">VA Appeals &amp; Adjudication</a> (principal anchor at SD7). Sources: Pub. L. 115-55 at <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/115th-congress/house-bill/2288">H.R. 2288</a>; 38 U.S.C. § 5107, § 5103A, Chs. 71-72.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#forever-gi-bill">Forever GI Bill</a> (Harry W. Colmery Act) — 15-year time limit eliminated for post-2013 separations</h2>
</header>
<p>The Harry W. Colmery Veterans Educational Assistance Act of 2017 (Pub. L. 115-48) eliminated the 15-year time limit on Chapter 33 GI Bill use for veterans who separated on or after January 1, 2013, made Purple Heart recipients fully eligible for Chapter 33 (irrespective of length of service), and made other expansions including STEM scholarships. The "Forever" provision structurally enables non-traditional / older veteran returners — a population characteristic of PA-3's veteran community — to access undergraduate or graduate education on Chapter 33 without time-bar foreclosure.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/education-benefits/">VA Education Benefits</a> (principal anchor at SD3). Sources: Pub. <span class="changelog-entry__date">June 6, 2018 (MISSION Act enacted; Pub. L. 115-182); June 6, 2019 (Veterans Community Care Program effective)</span>
<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MISSION Act — Veterans Community Care Program (VCCP) consolidated community-care architecture</h2>
</header>
<p>The VA MISSION Act of 2018 (Pub. L. 115-182; 38 U.S.C. § 1703) consolidated the Choice Program and other community-care authorities into the Veterans Community Care Program (VCCP). Eligibility criteria: VA cannot provide care; service not offered at VA facility; drive time / wait time exceeds standards (30-minute drive / 20-day wait for primary care and mental health; 60-minute drive / 28-day wait for specialty care); veteran and provider agree care is in veteran's best medical interest; care from non-VA provider is in best medical interest. <strong>VCCP's PA-3 partners include Penn Medicine, Jefferson Health, and Temple Health under Veterans Choice Program networks.</strong> The MISSION Act community-care architecture is the structural framework against which MC-04's GAO unimplemented-recommendation finding and MC-11's Dole Act community-care expansion both operate.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/vha/">VA Healthcare (VHA)</a> (principal anchor at SD1). Sources: Pub. L. 115-182 at <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/115th-congress/senate-bill/2372">S. 2372</a>; 38 U.S.C. § 1703.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">PACT Act — toxic-exposure presumption pathway + March 5, 2024 enrollment acceleration</h2>
</header>
<p>The Sergeant First Class Heath Robinson Honoring our Promise to Address Comprehensive Toxics Act of 2022 (PACT Act; Pub. L. 117-168) expanded VHA enrollment to all toxic-exposure veterans and established a broad presumption pathway for compensation claims tied to burn-pit, Agent Orange, and other documented exposures. Effective <strong>March 5, 2024</strong>, the phased enrollment schedule was eliminated; all eligible toxic-exposure veterans may enroll directly without first applying for disability benefits. <strong>The PACT Act drove the FY2024-FY2025 claims-volume surge that produced the January 2024 backlog peak documented in MC-01, the adjudication-guidance gaps documented in MC-07, and the substantive enrollment expansion at SD1.</strong> Substantive design: pro-veteran statutory expansion. Operational consequence: claims-volume surge stress-tested the VBA processing architecture and the BVA appeals pipeline simultaneously.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/vha/">VA Healthcare (VHA)</a> (SD1 care-delivery side, MC44); <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/vba-disability/">VBA Disability/Pension/VR&amp;E</a> (SD2 compensation-claim side, MC44). Sources: Pub. L. 117-168 at <a href="https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/senate-bill/3373">S. 3373</a>; VA implementation guidance for March 5, 2024 enrollment acceleration.</p>

<p>The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA; Pub. <h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC-11 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act</h2>
</header>
<p>The Senator Elizabeth Dole 21st Century Veterans Healthcare and Benefits Improvement Act (Pub. L. 118-210, 2025) expanded VA community-care provisions on top of the MISSION Act framework. Key provisions include: <strong>elimination of a secondary-physician-review step in "best medical interest" community-care determinations</strong> (effective May 2025); expanded caregiver support program access; expanded community-care mandates. <strong>The Dole Act adds a new statutory layer above the MISSION Act community-care architecture</strong> — substantive community-care expansion <em>and</em> additional administrative implementation obligations on top of MISSION Act recommendations that remain unimplemented per MC-04. Substantive design: pro-veteran statutory expansion. Operational implication: implementation capacity is the binding constraint, not statutory authority.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/veterans-affairs/sub-domains/vha/">VA Healthcare (VHA)</a> (principal anchor at SD1, community-care expansion). Sources: Pub. L. 118-210; U.S. Military MISSION Act guide, May 2026.</p>

OIG follow-up on the April 2025 report initiated <strong>December 29, 2025</strong>; public confirmation of complete corrective action has not been found as of May 2026 verification date. <strong>G24-SD2-01 remains a documented ongoing gap</strong> — the federal regulatory architecture acknowledged the adjudication-guidance failure, committed to corrective action, and the subsequent OIG follow-up documented continued accuracy problems on the nonpresumptive-conditions track where the operational delivery friction sits.</p>

<span class="changelog-entry__date">January 2026 (backlog data); March 2026 (processing-time data)</span>
<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC-01 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — VBA disability claims backlog ~100,115 (76.0% reduction from January 2024 peak)</h2>
</header>
<p>The VBA disability claims backlog (claims pending &gt;125 days) peaked at approximately <strong>417,855 in January 2024</strong> — a decade high driven by PACT Act volume surge — and fell to approximately <strong>134,009 by September 2025</strong> (67.9% reduction), then further to approximately <strong>100,115 by January 2026</strong> (<strong>76.0% total reduction from January 2024 peak</strong>; 25.3% additional reduction September 2025 → January 2026). Total pending claims (full inventory): approximately 551,895 as of January 2026; pending claims peaked at approximately 1.1 million in November 2023 and fell to approximately 626,653 by September 2025. <strong>Average processing time approximately 75.7 days as of March 2026</strong> (updated from approximately 81.1 days as of November 2025). VBA staffing fell from approximately 21,908 to 19,804 during the improvement period; VA reinstated mandatory overtime for claims processors in May 2025 after ending it in July 2024. <strong>The largest sustained backlog reduction in VBA history; G24-SD2-02 trajectory documented.</strong> Throughput improvement is substantive and durable; the underlying adjudication-quality gap on nonpresumptive conditions (MC-07) remains a separate dimension.</p>
