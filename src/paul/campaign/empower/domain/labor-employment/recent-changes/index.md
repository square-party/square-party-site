# Recent Changes — Labor &amp; Employment

<p class="domain-lead">Seven substantive Material Changes plus supplementary historical context have reshaped the Labor &amp; Employment architecture across 2024-2026. The verification cycle's aggregate finding — D10-Thread A formal-program-to-actual-benefit gap operates across all seven SDs with mechanically distinct gap-producing features per SD; D10-Thread B PRIMARY at SD4 (anchor institution subcontracting structure) plus SECONDARY at SD7 (community-hiring passdown); D10-Thread C subcontracting + worker misclassification + informal economy as three documented mechanisms; the four-dimensional anchor accountability framework architecturally complete after D10 (D7 real estate + D8 procurement + D9 fiscal + D10 employment via subcontracting structure); the triple-held-open-at-magnitude-level finding (G7-SD1-03 + D8-Q2 + D10-Q1) as project-level methodology validation evidence — is composed of these events. <strong>The most consequential structural change in the 2025-2026 administrative window is MC01 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ofccp">OFCCP</a>/EO 11246 elimination</strong> — the federal government's institutional acknowledgment that systemic enforcement (not individual charges) is the appropriate tool for large employers has been removed from the administrative landscape at the EO 11246 level, eliminated rather than reduced. <strong>The most consequential PA-state-level change is MC05 + MC06</strong> — the PA UC Trust Fund's below-solvency status (10+ years to full solvency) activating the statutory benefit reduction that froze the maximum weekly benefit at $605/week (down approximately 29% from the $854 prior-method baseline). <strong>The most consequential federal regulatory change at the NLRB-administrative level is MC03</strong> — the February 2026 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nlrb">NLRB</a> final rule formally codifying the narrow "direct and immediate control" joint-employer standard, closing the one legal mechanism that could have required Penn / Drexel / Temple to bargain directly over their subcontracted workforce's wages. <strong>MC07 D10-Q2 wage theft scale in PA-3 HELD-OPEN maintained</strong> per Phase 3 three-search budget protocol — methodology validation evidence accompanying the structural-mechanism documentation.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><em>Bostock v. Clayton County</em>, 590 U.S. 644 — Title VII sex protections cover sexual orientation and gender identity</h2>
</header>
<p>The U.S. Supreme Court in <em>Bostock v. Clayton County</em>, 590 U.S. 644 (2020) held that <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#title-vii">Title VII</a> of the Civil Rights Act's prohibition of "sex" discrimination encompasses discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation and gender identity. The decision provides the constitutional anchor for the federal anti-discrimination employment floor's coverage of LGBTQ+ workers at PA-3 employers with 15+ employees. <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#phrc">PHRC</a> has interpreted the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#phra">PHRA</a> at 43 P.S. § 951 et seq. (4-employee threshold) to extend parallel coverage through administrative interpretation; current PHRC interpretive position is F-flagged at F10-SD5-01. The <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pfpo">Philadelphia Fair Practices Ordinance</a> (Phila. Code Ch. 9-1100; 1-employee threshold) provides explicit sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, and domestic or sexual violence victim status protections.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/labor-employment/sub-domains/anti-discrimination/">Anti-Discrimination in Employment</a>.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Pandemic Unemployment Assistance (PUA) program — empirical floor for D10-Q3 gig classification scale</h2>
</header>
<p>The CARES Act (March 2020) created the Pandemic Unemployment Assistance program providing UC coverage to workers classified as independent contractors and traditionally excluded from state UC systems. The program operated through September 2021, generating state-administrative-data quality documentation of the gig workforce population at scale. Pennsylvania's <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pua">PUA</a> recipient framework confirmed hundreds of thousands of PA workers covered during 2020-2021; the PA-3-specific geographic subdivision was not retrieved within Phase 3 three-search budget. PUA's expiration in September 2021 restored the pre-pandemic independent-contractor exclusion under the PA UC Law's employment definition at 43 P.S. § 753(l). <strong>D10-Q3 best-estimate framing per substructure §8 with PUA dataset providing empirical floor</strong>: the PUA program quantified the exclusion that had been structurally invisible to PA UC data systems before 2020 and returned to invisibility after September 2021.</p>

Fifth Circuit appeal filed by Biden DOL February 2025; Trump DOL expected not to pursue. <em>Administrative vulnerability: LOW at the current $35,568 threshold level.</em></p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC01 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — OFCCP/EO 11246 elimination at federal contractors including PA-3 anchor employers</h2>
</header>
<p>Executive Order 14173 ("Ending Illegal Discrimination and Restoring Merit-Based Opportunity"), signed January 21, 2025, **revoked Executive Order 11246 entirely**. Secretary's Order 03-2025 (January 24, 2025) ordered OFCCP to immediately cease and desist all investigative and enforcement activity under EO 11246 and placed Section 503 and VEVRAA activity in abeyance pending further guidance. OFCCP was reduced from approximately 479 staff across 55 offices to approximately 50 employees in 4 regional locations. All pending compliance reviews were administratively closed. Section 503 (disability) and VEVRAA (veterans) enforcement resumed July 2, 2025 under Secretary's Order 08-2025. The FY2026 OBBBA (One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025; P.L. 119-21) eliminates OFCCP funding entirely pending Congressional action, with OFCCP operating under a continuing resolution. <strong>Penn, Temple, Drexel, CHOP, and Jefferson remain subject to Section 503 and VEVRAA enforcement but the EO 11246 race/sex/national origin affirmative action and non-discrimination obligations — the primary structural enforcement tool for addressing racial discrimination at large federal contractors — are gone.</strong> Administrative vulnerability status: EO 11246 enforcement has been eliminated rather than merely reduced. G10-SD1-03 and G10-SD5-03 confidence upgraded from MEDIUM to HIGH. The compounding D8 MC02 8(a) race-neutral restructuring (SBA formal guidance January 22, 2026; approximately 65 admissions in 2025 vs. hundreds historically; 1,000+ participants suspended January 2026) operates in the same administrative window — both race-conscious structural programs eliminated or race-neutralized simultaneously.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC05 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — PA UC maximum weekly benefit $605/week frozen under solvency trigger</h2>
</header>
<p>The prior-method figure of $854/week cited as the PA UC maximum weekly benefit is a prior-method baseline figure; the <strong>current operative maximum weekly benefit is $605/week, effective January 1, 2026</strong>. PA Bulletin official notice confirms that the trigger percentage fell below the 250% solvency threshold as of July 1, 2025, activating the statutory reduction provision. <strong>The 3.2% solvency reduction applies to all benefit payments.</strong> Standard 17 governmental-score updating: $854/week preserved as the acknowledged prior-method baseline; $605/week is the current operative maximum. The comparator: the current maximum represents approximately a 29% reduction from the prior-method figure, materially affecting the benefit-adequacy analysis (G10-SD6-03). For workers who were previously protected by the $854 ceiling, the downward adjustment increases income-replacement pressure during unemployment spells; for low-wage workers whose weekly wages are below the maximum threshold, the replacement-rate analysis is unchanged.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC03 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — Joint-employer narrow standard formally reinstated by February 2026 NLRB final rule</h2>
</header>
<p>In February 2026, the Trump-era NLRB issued a <strong>final rule formally withdrawing the Biden 2023 joint-employer rule and reinstating the traditional narrow joint-employer standard</strong> ("direct and immediate control" over essential terms and conditions of employment). The Biden 2023 rule had been vacated by E.D. Tex. in March 2024 in <em>Chamber of Commerce of the United States v. NLRB</em>; the 2026 NLRB final rule formally codifies the narrow standard by rule rather than leaving it in a litigation-vacatur posture. The narrow "direct and immediate control" standard is now formally operative by final rule. <strong>This directly affects the Penn/Aramark joint-employer theory</strong>: under the narrow standard, Penn cannot be compelled to bargain over wages for the Aramark workers in its dining halls unless Penn exercises direct and immediate control over their essential terms and conditions of employment. The February 2026 NLRB final rule formally reinstating the narrow joint-employer standard <strong>closes the door on the one legal mechanism that could have required Penn to bargain directly over its subcontracted workforce's wages</strong>. D10-Q1 held-open at magnitude maintained. T10-SD4-03 / F10-SD4-03 RESOLVED.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/labor-employment/sub-domains/labor-relations/">Labor Relations and Collective Bargaining</a> (principal anchor at SD4 §2 joint employer entry). Sources: NLRB February 2026 final rule; <em>Chamber of Commerce of the United States v. NLRB</em>, E.D. Tex. March 2024.</p>
