# Recent Changes — Commerce &amp; Industry

<p class="domain-lead">Eight substantive Material Changes plus supplementary historical context have reshaped the Commerce &amp; Industry architecture across 2023-2026. The verification cycle's aggregate finding — D8-Thread A formal-program-to-actual-benefit gap operating across all seven SDs with mechanically distinct gap-producing features per SD; D8-Thread B commercial-displacement parallel at SD4 and SD6 (MC14 carry-forward from D7 SD4); D8-Thread C anchor procurement as third accountability dimension completing the Standard 10.B triple-role finding at SD3 (D7 real estate + D8 procurement + D9 fiscal + D10 employment); the triple-held-open-at-magnitude finding (G7-SD1-03 + D8-Q2 + D10-Q1) as project-level methodology validation evidence — is composed of these events. <strong>The most consequential federal-regulatory change in the 2025-2026 administrative window is MC01 § 1071 2026 Final Rule</strong> — the public accountability data infrastructure that would have made the racial small business lending gap visible at the lender-and-tract level will operate, but at materially narrower scope than the 2023 rule contemplated, with compliance pushed to January 2028. <strong>The most consequential set-aside-program change is MC02 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sba">SBA</a> <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#8a">8(a)</a> race-neutral restructuring</strong> — the program most explicitly designed for racial equity in federal contracting has been formally race-neutralized, with program admissions contracting from hundreds in a typical year to approximately 65 in 2025 and 1,000+ existing participants suspended in January 2026. <strong>The most consequential place-based-investment change is MC04 / MC05 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#obbba">OBBBA</a> permanence</strong> — both <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#qoz">QOZ</a> and <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nmtc">NMTC</a> are now indefinite statutory authorizations, with a QOZ new-designation round effective January 1, 2027 that may materially reshape the PA-3 tract landscape. <strong>The most consequential CRA-and-CDFI change is MC06 / MC07</strong> — the 1995 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cra">CRA</a> framework continues to govern all banks following the 2023 Final Rule rescission posture; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cdfi-fund">CDFI Fund</a> FY 2026 level funding ($324M) was restored over the President's initial budget cut, but ~$298M of FY 2025 appropriations remain frozen by <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#omb">OMB</a>. <strong>The most consequential local-procurement change is MC03</strong> — Philadelphia's most recent <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#oeo">OEO</a> Disparity Study (FY 2021, published January 25, 2023) is now ~3 fiscal years old, raising a live <em>Croson</em> constitutional-currency question for the program's race-conscious dimensions. <strong>D8-Q1 (QOZ investment-vs-extraction)</strong> and <strong>D8-Q2 (anchor procurement commitment-vs-actual-spend)</strong> HOMs maintained per Phase 3 three-search budget protocol — methodology validation evidence accompanying the structural-mechanism documentation.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><em>City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.</em>, 488 U.S. 469 — constitutional disparity-study predicate for MBE/WBE programs</h2>
</header>
<p>The U.S. Supreme Court in <em>City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.</em>, 488 U.S. 469 (1989) held that state and local race-conscious set-aside programs are subject to strict scrutiny under the Equal Protection Clause, requiring a compelling government interest (typically demonstrated by a methodologically sound disparity study showing statistically significant racial disparity in contract awards relative to availability of qualified minority firms) and narrow tailoring. <em>Croson</em> is the constitutional predicate for Philadelphia's OEO MBE/WBE program; the program's constitutional validity depends on the adequacy and currency of Philadelphia's most recent disparity study. The MC03 disparity-study-currency concern flows directly from <em>Croson</em>: a stale or methodologically inadequate study leaves the race-conscious dimensions of the OEO program constitutionally vulnerable.</p>

Sources: <em>City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.</em>, 488 U.S. 469 (1989).</p>

The Standard 11 administrative-vulnerability concentration in SD2 is structurally an <em>Adarand</em>-line product.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) — natural experiment quantifying the racial small business credit gap</h2>
</header>
<p>The <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ppp">Paycheck Protection Program</a> (CARES Act 2020; subsequent appropriations) deployed SBA-backed forgivable loans through existing 7(a) lender networks at emergency scale. The racial-gap finding is documented at HIGH confidence: predominantly Black ZIP codes received fewer early loans (Chernenko / Scharfstein, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nber">NBER</a> WP 29748, February 2022; published in <em>Journal of Financial Economics</em> vol. 160 (2024)); Black-owned businesses received smaller amounts and were pushed toward fintech channels carrying higher fees; Fairlie (NBER WP 27462) documented a 41% decline in Black business ownership in the early months of the pandemic. <strong>PPP operated as a documented natural experiment confirming that the structural access gap is embedded in the delivery infrastructure, not merely in program parameters</strong> — under conditions of maximum policy intent for broad equitable access, the existing SBA architecture reproduced the documented racial gaps. The <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cdfi">CDFI</a> PPP performance differential (CDFI-originated PPP loans reached a higher share of Black-owned businesses than bank-originated loans, but CDFI scale was insufficient to close the access gap) is the analytical bridge from PPP to the SD7 CDFI Both/And finding.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>. Sources: Fairlie NBER WP 27462; Chernenko / Scharfstein NBER WP 29748 (February 2022); Chernenko, Kaplan, Sarkar, Scharfstein NBER WP 31172 (May 2023) using 2020 SBCS data.</p>
</article>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><em>Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA</em> — 8(a) racial presumption held unconstitutional as applied</h2>
</header>
<p>The U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Tennessee in <em>Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA</em>, No. 2:20-cv-00041 (E.D. Tenn. 2023) held that the SBA 8(a) program's racial presumption of social disadvantage was unconstitutional as applied. The court enjoined SBA from applying the racial presumption; SBA responded by issuing revised 8(a) eligibility guidance requiring individual case-by-case demonstration of social disadvantage in lieu of racial presumption. <em>Ultima Services</em> is the litigation event that produced the structural change later formalized by MC02 (SBA's January 22, 2026 formal race-neutral guidance) and is part of the same constitutional posture that, in the OFCCP context, produced the MC01 EO 11246 elimination cross-referenced at <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/labor-employment/sub-domains/anti-discrimination/">D10 SD5</a>.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><em>Texas Bankers Association v. CFPB</em> — § 1071 implementing-rule litigation predicate</h2>
</header>
<p><em>Texas Bankers Association v. CFPB</em>, No. 7:23-cv-00144 (S.D. Tex. 2023) was the primary litigation vehicle challenging the CFPB's 2023 § 1071 Final Rule (88 Fed. Reg. 35150, March 2023). The S.D. Texas court issued preliminary injunctive relief staying the rule's implementation; the litigation, together with <em>Monticello Banking</em> and <em>RBFC</em> cases, produced compliance-date extensions and, ultimately, the regulatory posture that culminated in MC01 — the CFPB's substantially narrowed 2026 Final Rule published May 1, 2026. The litigation history is the procedural reason the § 1071 accountability data infrastructure has not yet operated; <em>Rise Economy v. Vought</em> in D.C. district court is the current primary vehicle for civil-rights-advocate challenges favoring the broader 2023 framework.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC08 — PA Act 122 of 2022 LLC annual reporting requirement effective</h2>
</header>
<p>Pennsylvania Act 122 of 2022 (effective January 1, 2025) replaced Pennsylvania's decennial report system with a new annual reporting requirement. <strong>LLCs must file an Annual Report (DSCB:15-146) by September 30 each year</strong>; annual fee $7 for LLCs. First reports were required by September 30, 2025 for entities formed before 2025; full enforcement begins with 2027 reports. The recurring $7/year compliance obligation is modest in absolute terms, but it adds to the compliance stack facing early-stage PA-3 small businesses alongside the $125 LLC formation fee, registered-agent requirements, BIRT compliance, and BPL renewal. <strong>G8-SD1-02 formation-cost-barrier finding</strong>: the formation pathway's cost and complexity concentrate their impact on the lowest-capital, earliest-stage businesses — the entry point for closing the racial business ownership gap.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC06 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — CRA 2023 Final Rule rescission; 1995 framework continues to govern all banks</h2>
</header>
<p>The OCC, Federal Reserve Board, and FDIC's 2023 Final Rule modernizing the Community Reinvestment Act (88 Fed. Reg. 228) was stayed by preliminary injunction March 29, 2024 (N.D. Texas) and never took effect. <strong>On March 28, 2025 the agencies announced intent to rescind the 2023 Final Rule</strong>; on July 16, 2025 the agencies issued a joint NPR to formally rescind the 2023 rule and reinstate the 1995 CRA regulations. As of May 2026, the <strong>1995 CRA framework continues to govern all banks</strong>; the expanded assessment area, LMI evaluation criteria, and small business lending accountability mechanisms of the 2023 rule are not operative. <strong>G8-SD7-02 CRA small-business-lending-desert persistence finding is confirmed structural under the operative framework</strong>; the regulatory reform that would have expanded LMI accountability has been formally rescinded. CRA compliance can continue to be achieved through a limited number of high-profile loans rather than systematic corridor-level access expansion.</p>

<p>The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA; P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) made the <strong>Qualified Opportunity Zone program permanent</strong>: new designation round effective January 1, 2027 (10-year designation period); governors designate new zones by July 1, 2026; Treasury and IRS were finalizing new-round implementation procedures as of April 2026; CDFI Fund opened public comment on the new nomination tool with comments due May 5, 2026. The new-round designations may change the PA-3 QOZ tract landscape materially by January 2027.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC05 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — OBBBA makes NMTC program permanent</h2>
</header>
<p>OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025) made the <strong>New Markets Tax Credit (NMTC) program permanent</strong> (indefinite statutory authorization). The FY 2026 Consolidated Appropriations Act (February 3, 2026) maintains level funding. The CY 2024-2025 double-round NMTC allocation award announcement was delayed due to the federal government shutdown through November 2025. <strong>NMTC, unlike QOZ, requires deployment in LIC tracts</strong> (census tracts with ≥20% poverty rate or median income ≤80% of AMI) <strong>and is structured for direct community benefit</strong>; where it reaches, it produces documented community economic development outcomes — TRF NMTC-financed full-service grocery stores in previously food-desert PA-3 census tracts are operative project-level examples (cross-reference <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/economic-development-zones/#nmtc-west-philly-grocery">SD4 Profile 2 Cobbs Creek</a>). The structural gap is in scale and reach — not all PA-3 LIC tracts receive NMTC investment; the competitive-allocation mechanism means project distribution depends on CDE strategy and capacity.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC02 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — SBA 8(a) race-neutral restructuring formalized</h2>
</header>
<p>On January 22, 2026 the Small Business Administration issued <strong>formal guidance formalizing full race-neutral administration of the 8(a) Business Development program</strong>: no applicant may be denied or given presumptive preference based solely on race; all applicants must submit individualized narratives with supporting documentation demonstrating social disadvantage on a case-by-case basis. This formalized an administrative posture the SBA had been operating under since February 2025 following <em>Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA</em>. <strong>Program contraction is dramatic</strong>: only approximately 65 companies were admitted to the 8(a) program in 2025 (vs. hundreds in a typical year); SBA issued a data call to all 4,300+ existing 8(a) participants in December 2025 and <strong>suspended 1,000+ participants in January 2026</strong> for failing to respond. The orientation-mismatch gap (G8-SD2-01) is compounded by the program's structural narrowing. G8-SD2-03 confidence revised HIGH (from MEDIUM); the contingency framing has materialized as confirmed structural change. The compounding D10 MC01 OFCCP EO 11246 elimination (cross-reference <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/labor-employment/sub-domains/anti-discrimination/">D10 SD5</a>) operates in the same administrative window — both race-conscious structural programs (OFCCP affirmative-action reviews and 8(a) set-asides) eliminated or race-neutralized simultaneously.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a> (principal anchor at SD2 §1, §3 8(a) entries). Sources: <em>Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA</em> (E.D. Tenn. 2023); SBA formal guidance January 22, 2026; sba.gov 8(a) program documentation.</p>

<span class="changelog-entry__date">May 1, 2026 (CFPB 2026 Final Rule published)</span>
<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC01 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — § 1071 2026 Final Rule narrows scope; January 2028 compliance</h2>
</header>
<p>The CFPB published a substantially revised § 1071 Final Rule on <strong>May 1, 2026</strong>, superseding the 2023 rule and its compliance-date extensions. The 2026 Final Rule narrows § 1071 coverage materially: <strong>origination threshold increases from 100 to 1,000 covered credit transactions per two consecutive years</strong>; merchant cash advances, agricultural lending, and loans under $1,000 are expressly excluded from covered transactions; <strong>small business revenue definition reduced from $5M to $1M in gross annual revenue</strong>; a <strong>single compliance date of January 1, 2028</strong> applies to all covered institutions (eliminating the tiered structure). The litigation that prompted the earlier stays (<em>Texas Bankers Association v. CFPB</em>, <em>Monticello Banking</em>, <em>RBFC</em>) is substantially mooted by the rule narrowing. <em>Rise Economy v. Vought</em> in D.C. district court is the primary vehicle for challenges from civil rights advocates favoring the broader 2023 framework. <strong>Analytical consequence</strong>: the § 1071 data infrastructure mandate will operate, but at narrower scope than the 2023 rule contemplated — fewer lenders covered, fewer data points collected, MCAs excluded from coverage. <strong>The discouragement gap finding (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sbcs">SBCS</a> 2022 ~37% non-application rate for Black-owned applicants vs. ~14% white-owned) remains outside § 1071's accountability reach regardless of rule scope</strong>; the frame for G8-SD1-03 shifts from "contingent gap pending rule implementation" to "operating gap under a narrowed data-collection regime effective January 2028." The accountability gap analysis at the corridor level remains accurate until the data flows post-2028, and the 2026 Final Rule's narrowed coverage limits the eventual accountability regime relative to the 2023 rule.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a> (principal anchor at SD1 §1, §2 ECOA / § 1071 entries); <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a> (secondary anchor at SD7 §2 § 1071 entry). Sources: CFPB 2026 Final Rule (published May 1, 2026); CFPB 2023 Final Rule (88 Fed. Reg. 35150, March 2023); Final Rule October 2, 2025 (Regulation B, Docket CFPB-2025-0040); <em>Rise Economy v. Vought</em>, D.C. district court.</p>
