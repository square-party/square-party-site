# Physical Infrastructure

<p class="domain-lead">Physical infrastructure in PA-3 is the systems that everyone uses regardless of attention paid: SEPTA's buses and trains, PWD's water and sewer, the Streets Department's roads and signals, the School District's buildings, Parks &amp; Recreation's land and pools, the Housing Authority's redevelopment, and the federal funding architecture flowing through all of them. Across the seven sub-domains analyzed here, the recurring structural finding is that federal floors and state implementation produce uneven coverage at the cumulative-network level — even when individual programs operate as designed at the project level. The cumulative racial disadvantage geography of PA-3 expresses itself in differential infrastructure burden: lead service line concentration in pre-1986 housing in low-income communities of color; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hin">High Injury Network</a> concentration with a 2.4× killed-or-seriously-injured disparity by <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pdph-uc-metric">PDPH Underserved Communities</a> tract; illegal dumping concentration in poorer neighborhoods with higher Black and Latino populations; school building condition concentration, with 85 SDP buildings rated poor or unsatisfactory; and tree canopy ranging from under 5% to over 45% with a 22-degree heat differential between hottest and coolest places in the city. The most distinctive federal-floor finding is the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ahera">AHERA</a> enforcement / federal-funding-silence intersection — the U.S. Attorney's June 2025 deferred prosecution agreement against the School District of Philadelphia operates as criminal-floor enforcement that no federal financing program supports. The most consequential near-term federal lever is <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#iija">IIJA</a> reauthorization (expiring September 30, 2026; no bill introduced as of April 2026), with reauthorization decisions over the next ~17 months affecting FY27–FY31 funding architecture across SEPTA, PWD, the Streets Department, SDP, PPR, the Housing Authority, and the airport.</p>

<aside class="layered-section layered-section--light" aria-label="Where there's traction">
<h2>Where there's traction</h2>
<p>Reauthorization of the IIJA is the single most consequential near-term federal lever. With authorizations expiring September 30, 2026 and no reauthorization bill introduced as of April 2026, decisions over the next ~17 months will determine FY27–FY31 funding levels, formula structures, competitive program design, and equity-targeting criteria for the federal flows that reach SEPTA, PWD, the Streets Department, the School District, Parks &amp; Recreation, the Housing Authority, and the airport. Federal House representation has direct levers on formula-program stability for direct-recipient flows; on equity-targeting criteria after the post-2025 retraction of EO 14008 (Justice40), EO 12898, and EO 14096; on capital program funding levels (Section 5309 CIG, BRIC, BIP, ORLP); and on protection of the Land and Water Conservation Fund's permanent $900 million annual appropriation under the Great American Outdoors Act — already protected by Congress's Interior and Environment Appropriations Subcommittee against the FY 2026 administration proposal of a 43% diversion.</p>
<p>Federal-state-local mandate stacks expose specific reform pathways. The most actionable federal-floor gap surfaced in the domain is the AHERA enforcement / federal-funding-silence intersection at SDP: federal AHERA criminal-floor enforcement obligation operates without a federal compliance-financing program. The June 2025 deferred prosecution agreement is in effect under judicial oversight; SDP's environmental management budget grew from $10.2 million in FY21 to $55.7 million in FY25 to meet enforcement, financed entirely from district resources and a $100 million Penn donation pledged in 2020. Federal House representation can advocate for federal AHERA compliance financing as new program development — agenda-setting work without a current legislative pathway, but a tractable structural ask grounded in documented enforcement.</p>
<p>State-level levers are operating. Pennsylvania's November 12, 2025 enacted FY 2025–26 budget — Day 135 of an unprecedented impasse — allocated $565 million toward closing the William Penn state-constitutional adequacy gap; Governor Shapiro's FY 2026–27 budget proposal (February 19, 2026) includes an additional $565 million plus a 1.75% Sales-and-Use-Tax allocation to the Pennsylvania Transportation Trust Fund starting July 1, 2027 (~$300–319.6 million annually for SEPTA and other PA transit). The FY 2026–27 budget is not yet enacted as of late April 2026 (deadline June 30, 2026). Federal representation has indirect levers on these through congressional delegation coordination with state legislators and through federal-funding conditions that affect state behavior.</p>
<p>Local operations are expanding. Mayor Parker's March 2024 Vision Zero executive order produced the Vision Zero Action Plan 2030 (November 2025), committing to safety upgrades on every mile of the High Injury Network by 2030. Roosevelt Boulevard automated speed enforcement (in place since 2020) demonstrates what aligned federal-floor and city operations can achieve: a 95%+ reduction in speeding violations, a 21% reduction in fatal/serious-injury crashes, a 50% reduction in pedestrian-involved crashes. The 2024 Sanitation/Streets reorganization, the One Philly Illegal Dumping Task Force (November 2025), the SEPTA $394 million capital-to-operating buffer (September 2025) and additional $219.9 million flexed for Silverliner IV (November 2025), and PWD's first lead service line replacement pilot (~1,000 lines budgeted for 2026 in North and West Philadelphia) all represent recent operational expansion within existing federal and state architectures. The Eastwick HESCO project ($1.38 million federal funding via Rep Mary Gay Scanlon, PA-5, October 2024) demonstrates that cross-jurisdictional inter-district congressional coordination is operationally possible — and is the model for similar coordination on other PA-3 federal-funding pathways that cross PA-4 (Wissahickon) or federal-management (John Heinz NWR) boundaries.</p>

<aside class="layered-section layered-section--dark" aria-label="What's at stake">
<h2>What's at stake</h2>
<p>The cumulative racial disadvantage geography of PA-3 expresses itself in differential infrastructure burden across five of seven sub-domains, in patterns that track the same underlying demographic geography in each case. Lead service line concentration runs through pre-1986 rowhomes in low-income communities of color in North and West Philadelphia. The High Injury Network puts 137 of approximately 198 HIN miles in tracts scoring highest on the PDPH Underserved Communities metric, with killed-or-seriously-injured rates 2.4 times higher in those tracts than in the lowest-UC tracts. Illegal dumping concentrates in poorer neighborhoods with higher proportions of Black and Latino residents per Billy Penn's 2022 documentation. The 85 SDP buildings rated poor or unsatisfactory concentrate in older buildings disproportionately located in the Cores; the AHERA findings touch 31 schools across 2015–2023, with 7 schools named in the criminal information. Tree canopy ranges from under 5% in some neighborhoods to over 45% in others; the Philly Tree Plan (2023) documents a 22-degree heat differential between the hottest and coolest places in the city. These are not five independent patterns. They are the same demographic geography expressing itself through five different infrastructure systems.</p>
<p>The structural mismatches between formal provision and actual experience are layered and traceable. Federal floor protections (Title VI environmental justice review, ADA Title II accessibility, AHERA, SDWA, CWA, RCRA, NFIP, Stafford Act) operate at the project level and produce uneven coverage at the cumulative-network level. The post-2025 retraction of Justice40 — with all ten EPA regional environmental justice offices closed and EJScreen public access restricted — has thinned the federal administrative architecture for need-based equity targeting without changing the underlying statutory floor. The William Penn ruling (final July 2023) declared Pennsylvania's school funding system constitutionally inadequate; Pennsylvania funds schools at a lower percentage from state sources than all but six other states; approximately $3.8 billion of the original $4.6–6.2 billion adequacy gap remains after the 2024–25 and 2025–26 budgets' partial response. SEPTA's structural state-match vulnerability after Act 89's 2022 transition (which moved $400 million of dedicated PA Turnpike transit funding to the state General Fund) is the architectural cause of the 2024–2026 fiscal crisis — federal Section 5307 formula apportionment continued throughout, but the state operating-support layer underneath collapsed under annual budget negotiation pressure.</p>
<p>The fiscal magnitudes are not small. PWD's lead service line replacement is estimated at $500 million in total cost for 16,805 confirmed lead lines plus an unknown share of 351,514 lines of unknown material. SDP's "Accelerating Opportunity" Facilities Master Plan calls for $3 billion over ten years, with $2 billion requested from state and philanthropic sources; the District's overall maintenance backlog is approximately $7 billion per PFT President Steinberg's 2024 estimate. SEPTA's projected state of good repair backlog is $5.1 billion, projected to grow to $10.2 billion over twelve years absent intervention. FDR Park's master plan implementation is $250 million, with $111 million invested since groundbreaking. Federal funding flows through P.L. 119-75 in February 2026 transferred or rescinded approximately $2.3 billion in IIJA funds mid-cycle, including ~$879 million from NEVI alone — demonstrating that even nominally stable program categories face mid-cycle reductions or program-horizon narrowing.</p>
<p>The constituent experience of these architectures is concrete, not abstract. The bus route eliminated and then restored by court order in the August–September 2025 SEPTA action. The lead service line in a North Philadelphia rowhome facing the LCRR/LCRI notification but no replacement until the 2026 pilot reaches that block. The HIN corridor where the family member walked to a bus stop. The dumping hot spot adjacent to a vacant lot. The asbestos-cited classroom. The heat-vulnerable tree-canopy-deficient neighborhood. The flood-vulnerable Eastwick rowhome facing the quadruple threat of Cobbs Creek and Darby Creek flooding, storm surge, and climate-driven sea-level rise. The structural representation question for PA-3 federal House representation is whether engagement bridges the federal-architecture-design level (reauthorization, program structure, equity criteria) and the constituent-experience level (specific projects, specific institutional gaps, specific sub-area patterns) — or operates primarily at one level. The Eastwick HESCO example shows the bridging is possible; gaps in equivalent attention to other PA-3 federal-funding pathways suggest the bridging is currently uneven.</p>
</aside>
