# Recent Changes — Physical Infrastructure

<p class="domain-lead">Sixteen substantive Material Changes plus supplementary historical context have reshaped the Physical Infrastructure architecture across 2024-2026. The verification cycle's aggregate finding — federal authorization stable through September 30, 2026 but with mid-cycle transfer/rescission risk demonstrated by P.L. 119-75 and OBBBA; federal-criminal-floor enforcement operating without compensating compliance financing across AHERA, SDWA, ADA Title II, and LCRR/LCRI; cumulative racial disadvantage geography expressing through infrastructure burden differential across SD2/SD3/SD4/SD5/SD6; the IIJA reauthorization inflection point at September 30, 2026 as the proximate near-term federal lever for all six substantive sub-domains — is composed of these events. <strong>The most consequential federal-architectural change is the IIJA reauthorization-trajectory uncertainty (MC-02)</strong> — no bill introduced as of May 2026 with September 30, 2026 expiration. <strong>The most consequential federal-administrative change is MC-03 Justice40 framework dismantling</strong> — EO 14008, EO 12898, and EO 14096 all revoked through 2025; all 10 EPA regional environmental justice offices closed. <strong>The most consequential federal-fiscal change is MC-01 P.L. 119-75 transfers</strong> — ~$2.3B in unobligated IIJA balances transferred February 3, 2026 ending the partial government shutdown; Reconnecting Communities Program cut to 15% of obligated FY26 level. <strong>The most consequential state-level changes are MC-04 SEPTA fiscal crisis</strong> (operating chronology June-December 2025) and <strong>MC-05 William Penn $565M legislative response</strong> (November 12, 2025 PA budget against ~$3.8B remaining adequacy gap). <strong>The most consequential local-level changes are MC-07 AHERA DPA against SDP</strong> (June 26, 2025; cross-domain principal anchor at D6 G6-SD4-02), <strong>MC-08 PWD lead service line architecture</strong> (511,000 service lines inventoried), <strong>MC-09 Vision Zero Action Plan 2030</strong> (released November 25, 2025), and the December 19, 2025 Sanitation Department creation. <strong>D13-Q1 IIJA reauthorization trajectory (G13-SD7-01)</strong> and the six held-open-at-magnitude items across the SDs are preserved per Phase 3 discipline — methodology validation evidence accompanying the structural-mechanism documentation.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Pennsylvania Act 89 of 2013 transit-funding architecture — the structural predicate for the 2024-2026 SEPTA fiscal crisis</h2>
</header>
<p>Pennsylvania Act 89 of 2013, signed by Governor Tom Corbett, restructured the state transit funding architecture: dedicated the full $450M annual PA Turnpike Commission payment to public transit; eliminated the $0.12/gallon state retail gas and diesel tax; raised the cap on the Oil Company Franchise Tax; established a multi-modal fund. <strong>The 2022 Act 89 transition</strong> reduced the PA Turnpike Commission's annual payment to PennDOT from $450M to $50M, with the $400M difference shifting to the state General Fund — converting a dedicated Turnpike-toll-funded transit subsidy into an annual General Fund appropriation subject to budget negotiation. This is the structural predicate (G13-SD1-03) for the 2024-2026 SEPTA fiscal crisis: the General Fund route exposes transit operating support to political negotiation in ways the dedicated Turnpike route did not.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/transit/">Transit &amp; Mobility</a> (principal anchor at SD1 §2). Sources: PA Act 89 of 2013; PA Department of Transportation; SEPTA primary sources.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">Great American Outdoors Act (GAOA) — LWCF permanent funding</h2>
</header>
<p>The Great American Outdoors Act of 2020 provided permanent mandatory $900M annual funding for the Land and Water Conservation Fund (under the John D. Dingell Jr. Conservation, Management, and Recreation Act of 2019). LWCF supports federal land acquisition (federal side) and state-side grants to states for parks and recreation acquisition and development (state side), funded primarily by Outer Continental Shelf oil and gas lease revenue. This statutory predicate is what allows Congress to protect LWCF FY 2026 funding against the Administration's proposed 43% diversion in FY 2026 (per LWCF Coalition praise, July 22, 2025).</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a> (principal anchor at SD6 §2). Sources: GAOA P.L. 116-152; 54 U.S.C. §§ 200301 et seq.; LWCF Coalition.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title"><em>William Penn School District v. Commonwealth</em> — PA's school funding system held unconstitutional</h2>
</header>
<p>The Pennsylvania Commonwealth Court in <em>William Penn School District v. Commonwealth</em>, 297 A.3d 1163 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2023), found Pennsylvania's school funding system unconstitutional under PA Const. art. III § 14 (the "thorough and efficient" clause) and ordered legislative remediation. The decision built on the PA Supreme Court's 2017 justiciability decision (170 A.3d 414 (Pa. 2017)) and the 2022 decision (282 A.3d 1247 (Pa. 2022)). Education Law Center December 2025 analysis: $4.6-6.2B initial adequacy gap; subsequent legislative budget processes have established multi-year funding response.</p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">OBBBA (P.L. 119-21) — IRA infrastructure program restructuring</h2>
</header>
<p>The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA; P.L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) restructured the IRA-funded portion of the federal infrastructure-program horizon. <h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC-08 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — PWD lead service line inventory and LCRI compliance architecture</h2>
</header>
<p>EPA promulgated the Lead and Copper Rule Improvements (LCRI) final rule on October 30, 2024 (effective December 30, 2024; compliance date November 1, 2027) — lead action level reduced from 15 ppb to 10 ppb; lead trigger level removed; service line replacement plans required. <strong>PWD lead service line inventory per WHYY December 2025 reporting:</strong> of 511,000 total service lines, <strong>16,805 confirmed lead, 157,823 confirmed lead-free, 351,514 of unknown material</strong>; approximately 1 in 20 (5%) of properties may have a lead service line; PWD has determined materials of ~85,000 previously-unknown lines since October 2024 launch of public Service Line Materials Map. ~480,000 letters covering ~360,000 properties sent December 2025. The 2026 pilot Service Line Replacement Program (~1,000 lead service lines in North and West Philadelphia) is the first PWD program to replace lines outside of planned water main construction or HELP loan requests. Total estimated cost of replacing all known lead service lines: ~$500M. <strong>Cross-reference D6 MC-06 — LCRI defended by EPA in *AWWA v. EPA* with Respondents' Brief filed February 20, 2026; D6 MC-06 is the cross-domain principal anchor for LCRI federal-architecture defense.</strong></p>

<h2 class="changelog-entry__title">MC-09 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — Vision Zero Action Plan 2030 released</h2>
</header>
<p>The Vision Zero Action Plan 2030 was released November 25, 2025 by OTIS, Vision Zero Philadelphia, PDPH, Department of Streets, and Office of Emergency Management — fulfilling Mayor Parker's March 2024 executive order. The plan documents the structural finding: <strong>12% of streets account for 80% of fatal/serious-injury crashes</strong> (the High Injury Network); <strong>PDPH Underserved Communities metric documents a KSI crash rate 2.4 times higher in highest-UC tracts than in lowest-UC tracts</strong>; approximately one-third (<strong>137 miles</strong>) of the HIN is located in highest-UC tracts, while only 14% (61 miles) is in lowest-UC tracts (G13-SD3-01). Traffic fatalities reached 120 in 2024 (down from 123 in 2023); 5-year average since 2020 more than 50% higher than 5-year average prior to 2020. Roosevelt Boulevard speed cameras (2020-) produced 95%+ reduction in speeding violations, 21% reduction in fatal/serious-injury crashes, and 50% reduction in pedestrian crashes; Complete Streets projects produced 34% fewer fatal/serious-injury crashes and 20% fewer total injury crashes. The Plan commits to safety upgrades on every mile of the HIN by 2030 plus an Intelligent Speed Assistance (ISA) pilot in the municipal fleet.</p>
<p class="changelog-entry__refs">Affects: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/roads/">Roads, Bridges &amp; Pedestrian Infrastructure</a> (principal anchor at SD3 §4).
