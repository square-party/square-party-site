<h2>The shape of the system</h2>

<p>The federal environmental statutory floor — <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#clean-air-act">CAA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cwa">CWA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sdwa">SDWA</a> with <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#lcrr-lcri">LCRR/LCRI</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cercla">CERCLA</a> with <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sara">SARA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#rcra">RCRA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#tsca">TSCA</a> with <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ahera">AHERA</a> and the lead-paint <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#rrp">RRP</a> rule, Title X <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hcda">HCDA</a>, the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#esa">Endangered Species Act</a>, the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#mbta">Migratory Bird Treaty Act</a>, CWA § 404, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nepa">NEPA</a>, and <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#title-vi">Title VI</a> — is comprehensive at the statutory layer and stable. Pennsylvania operates the substantive programs under federal primacy: <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#dep-pa">PA DEP</a> administers Title V permitting through Philadelphia Air Management Services delegation, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#act-2-pa">PA Act 2 of 1995</a> runs the voluntary brownfield-cleanup architecture, PA DEP <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#bsdw">BSDW</a> administers SDWA, the PA Lead Paint Abatement Act layers atop TSCA Title IV, and PA DEP's January 2026 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ej">EJ</a> Policy — the first update in more than 20 years — operationalizes PennEnviroScreen and an <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#oej">Office of Environmental Justice</a> at Special Deputy Secretary level. Philadelphia delivers permitting at <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pams">PAMS</a>, water and sewer service at <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pwd">PWD</a>, and lead-disclosure enforcement at the local code level. Six sub-domains organize the architecture: air; water and lead-service-line replacement; land, sites, and brownfields; built-environment hazards (lead paint, asbestos, radon); cumulative environmental burden and environmental justice; and conservation, public lands, and wildlife.</p>

<p>The administrative architecture above the floor has moved in both directions in 2025–2026 and the two directions are simultaneously operative. The federal layer is under documented disruption: the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ghg">GHG</a> endangerment finding was rescinded on February 18, 2026 (91 FR, Docket EPA-HQ-OAR-2025-0194), with five distinct petitions for review pending in the D.C. Circuit; EPA <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ord">ORD</a> was formally eliminated in July 2025 with approximately 500 of 1,540 positions retained at the successor OASES; Trump executive orders 14148, 14151, and 14173 (January 20–21, 2025) revoked the historical environmental-justice executive-order chain and <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ejscreen">EJScreen</a> was taken offline; approximately $20B in <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ira">IRA</a> environmental-justice grants were cancelled and approximately 50 EJ-focused EPA staff were terminated in August 2025. The PA state layer expanded in the same window: PA DEP's January 3, 2026 Final EJ Policy, $267,825,172 in RISE PA industrial-decarbonization grants announced April 28, 2026 (drawn from EPA Climate Pollution Reduction Grants), PWD's 2026 lead-service-line pilot in approximately 1,000 properties in North and West Philadelphia under the LCRI 2027–2037 mandatory replacement window. Both directions are real; neither cancels the other; the magnitude question — whether state expansion compensates for federal dismantlement, or federal rollback dominates state continuation — is empirically open through the 2026 gubernatorial cycle.</p>

<p>The dominant gap in PA-3 is structurally not between formal provision and its absence, but between formal provision and the practical constituent capacity to access, enforce, and benefit from it. Title V permit public-comment processes are formally open and technically inaccessible without facility-side regulatory counsel. The LCRI mandatory lead-service-line replacement is mediated through owner consent that renters — about 51% of PA-3 households — cannot independently authorize. PA Act 2's site-specific remediation standard permits residual contamination at commercial and industrial-use sites with institutional controls that PA DEP does not proactively inspect; enforcement is complaint-driven. AHERA management plans for <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sdp">SDP</a> buildings are formally available; in June 2025 the U.S. Attorney for EDPA filed a Deferred Prosecution Agreement against SDP for AHERA compliance failures across 31 buildings from 2015–2023 — the first criminal AHERA charges against any U.S. school district. Five mechanisms — historical redlining underlay, current-source emissions concentration, anchor footprint contributions, transportation-corridor proximity, and brownfield/Superfund site distribution — concentrate cumulative environmental burden in the North and West Philadelphia core sub-areas and the South/Southwest corridor along I-95 and I-76. The same five anchor institutions — Penn and Penn Medicine, Temple and Temple Health, Drexel, CHOP, and Jefferson Health — appear across the air, hazardous-waste, and built-environment-hazard regulatory dimensions, and the commitment-vs-actual gap between their published sustainability commitments and their measured compliance posture is empirically open at magnitude.</p>
