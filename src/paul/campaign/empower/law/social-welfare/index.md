# Legal text appendix — Public Benefits & Safety Net

<p class="domain-lead">The legal chain that produces PA-3's public-benefits and safety-net architecture, organized constitutional → federal → state → local. For quick definitions of acronyms (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#snap">SNAP</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#tanf">TANF</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ssdi">SSDI</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#obbba">OBBBA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hcv">HCV</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ehv">EHV</a>, CHC, CCDF, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#oaa">OAA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ffpsa">FFPSA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#eitc">EITC</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#wptc">WPTC</a>, etc.) and terms-of-art, see the <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/">glossary</a>. For the analytical treatment of how each instrument operates and where its gaps fall, see the eight <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/">sub-domain pages</a>.</p>

## Constitutional foundation

### U.S. Constitution

**Article I, § 8 — Spending Clause** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/articlei#section8)).<br>
Authorizes federal financial assistance to state and local programs subject to federal program requirements. The constitutional basis for the cooperative federal-state Medicaid program, TANF, SSI, SNAP, Title IV-E foster care, Title IV-D child support, McKinney-Vento, OAA, LIHEAP, and the entirety of D12's federal-floor architecture. Conditional-spending principles articulated in *South Dakota v. Dole*, 483 U.S. 203 (1987) govern state-plan submission requirements.<br>
*Cited in:* every D12 sub-domain.

**14th Amendment, § 1 — Equal Protection / Due Process** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentxiv)).<br>
Parental rights are recognized as fundamental liberty interests subject to procedural due process protections in dependency and termination proceedings. Equal protection grounds anti-discrimination architecture across federally-funded programs.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

### Key foundational cases

***Helvering v. Davis*, 301 U.S. 619 (1937)**.<br>
Upheld Title II of the Social Security Act, establishing the constitutional template for federal income-maintenance programs operating through dedicated payroll tax (FICA).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

***Steward Machine Co. v. Davis*, 301 U.S. 548 (1937)**.<br>
Upheld the federal-state cooperative structure of unemployment insurance, establishing the constitutional foundation for cooperative federal-state benefit programs more broadly.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

***South Dakota v. Dole*, 483 U.S. 203 (1987)**.<br>
Articulated the conditional-spending principles that govern TANF state-plan submission to HHS as the condition of receiving the federal block grant. Applied across the D12 federal-state cooperative architecture.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

***NFIB v. Sebelius*, 567 U.S. 519 (2012)**.<br>
Established Medicaid expansion as a state-option rather than a mandatory state requirement under the ACA. Pennsylvania's expansion election was effectuated by then-Governor Wolf via executive action on the new adult group, effective January 1, 2015.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

### Pennsylvania Constitution

**Article I, § 1 — Inherent rights** and **§ 28 — No discrimination** ([PA Constitution](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=00&amp;div=0&amp;chpt=1)).<br>
Provide state-level grounding for cooperative federal-state programs, alongside the general-welfare framework supporting state-level disability and aging supports.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

## Federal statutory layer

### Social Security Act titles

**Title II — OASDI**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 401–434 ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-II)).<br>
Funded through dedicated [FICA](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#fica) payroll tax (26 U.S.C. § 3101). Contributory social insurance, not means-tested. 2026 average retired-worker benefit: $2,071/month; disabled-worker benefit: $1,630/month in Pennsylvania. *Statutory stability: HIGH. Administrative vulnerability: MODERATE — formulas are fixed; service delivery (field offices, processing times, call-center capacity) is administratively variable.*<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

**Title IV-A — TANF**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 601–619, restructured from [AFDC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#afdc) by [PRWORA](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#prwora) 1996 (P.L. 104-193).<br>
Block grant to states; 60-month federal default time limit; work participation requirements. PA's Group 2 standard is $403/month for a family of three in Philadelphia County. The federal block-grant amount has been nominally fixed since 1996 — three decades of inflation erosion as a structural feature. *Statutory stability: HIGH. Administrative vulnerability: HIGH.*<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Title IV-B**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 620 et seq. ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-IV/part-B)).<br>
Authorizes the Stephanie Tubbs Jones Child Welfare Services program (Subpart 1) and Promoting Safe and Stable Families (Subpart 2).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Title IV-D**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 651 et seq. ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-IV/part-D)).<br>
Authorizes Child Support Enforcement. PA's Bureau of Child Support Enforcement administers the state IV-D program. Philadelphia OCSE operates within the First Judicial District of Pennsylvania.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Title IV-E**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 670 et seq. ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-IV/part-E)).<br>
Authorizes federal financial participation in foster-care maintenance payments, adoption assistance, kinship guardianship assistance, and the John H. Chafee Foster Care Program for Successful Transition to Adulthood. Amended by FFPSA (P.L. 115-123, 2018) to permit federal reimbursement for evidence-based prevention services to families at risk of foster-care entry.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Title XVI — [SSI](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ssi)**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1381–1385 ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-XVI)).<br>
Federal means-tested cash for aged, blind, or disabled persons with limited income and resources. 2026 Federal Benefit Rate: $994/month individual; $1,491/month couple. Resource limits remain $2,000 individual / $3,000 couple — set by statute and unindexed since 1989, structurally tightening eligibility as nominal asset values rise. *Statutory stability: HIGH. Administrative vulnerability: HIGH.*<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Title XIX — Medicaid**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1396 et seq. ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-7/subchapter-XIX)).<br>
Establishes mandatory and optional eligibility groups, mandatory and optional benefits, federal financial participation rules (FMAP), and managed-care authority. **Section 1915(c)** authorizes [HCBS](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hcbs) waivers; **Section 1934** authorizes the PACE program (implemented in Pennsylvania as LIFE). Approximately 2.99 million Pennsylvanians are enrolled as of mid-2025, including approximately 1.1 million in the Group VIII expansion (ages 19–64 to 138% FPL) and approximately 2.2 million in HealthChoices managed care.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Title XXI — [CHIP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#chip)**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1397aa et seq.<br>
Authorizes the Children's Health Insurance Program. PA CHIP extends children's coverage up to 319% FPL.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

### Other federal entitlement statutes

**[The Affordable Care Act](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#aca)**, P.L. 111-148 (2010).<br>
Added the new adult eligibility group (Group VIII), established MAGI-based eligibility methodology for non-disabled non-elderly populations, and required streamlined enrollment systems integrating with the Marketplace. Pennsylvania's expansion election was effectuated by then-Governor Wolf via executive action effective January 1, 2015.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

**The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)**, P.L. 119-21 (signed July 4, 2025).<br>
The most consequential statutory transformation of D12's federal floor in a generation. Phases new procedural and substantive constraints across 2026, 2027, and 2028 through Medicaid (Section 71107 6-month redetermination; Section 71119 community-engagement reporting; Section 71120 cost-sharing) and SNAP (ABAWD age extension to 64; parent-exemption tightening to youngest child under 14; veteran/homeless/former-foster-youth exemption removal; SUA restriction; TFP CPI-U-only adjustment; state administrative cost-share rising to 75% in FY 2027). Restricted qualified-immigrant categories effective October 1, 2026. CBO projected approximately $1 trillion federal Medicaid spending decrease and approximately 11.8 million coverage losses over FY 2025–FY 2034 per the July 2025 score; approximately $186 billion SNAP cuts and 2.4 million coverage losses.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/nutrition-assistance/">Nutrition Assistance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/cumulative-architecture/">Cumulative Architecture</a>.

**Food and Nutrition Act of 2008**, 7 U.S.C. §§ 2011 et seq. ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/chapter-51)).<br>
Authorizes SNAP. Benefits are 100% federally funded; administrative costs have historically been shared 50/50 federal-state, shifting to federal-25 / state-75 in FY 2027 under OBBBA. FY 2026 maximum monthly allotments (48 contiguous states): $298 HH-1; $546 HH-2; $785 HH-3; $994 HH-4; $1,183 HH-5; $1,421 HH-6.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/nutrition-assistance/">Nutrition Assistance</a>.

**Richard B. Russell National School Lunch Act and Child Nutrition Act of 1966**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1751 et seq. and §§ 1771 et seq.<br>
Authorize [NSLP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nslp), [SBP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sbp), [SFSP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sfsp), the [Community Eligibility Provision](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cep) (CEP) allowing schools with at least 25% identified students to offer free meals to all students, and [WIC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#wic). SDP operates as a CEP-participating district for the great majority of its schools.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/nutrition-assistance/">Nutrition Assistance</a>.

**26 U.S.C. § 32 — Earned Income Tax Credit** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/32)).<br>
Refundable credit operating through tax filing; benefit conditioned on earned income through a phase-in, plateau, and phase-out structure. Federal floor for the work-conditioned credit architecture. *Statutory stability: HIGH. Administrative vulnerability: MODERATE — IRS and VITA capacity drive effective uptake.*<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/cumulative-architecture/">Cumulative Architecture</a>.

**United States Housing Act of 1937**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 1437 et seq.<br>
Authorizes Public Housing and Housing Choice Vouchers (originally Section 8). Participating tenants pay 30% of adjusted monthly household income (or a minimum rent, currently $50 at PHA) toward rent; HUD covers the difference up to the Fair Market Rent.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Cranston-Gonzalez National Affordable Housing Act of 1990**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 12701 et seq.<br>
Authorizes HOME Investment Partnerships, administered federally by HUD CPD with state-allocation administered by [PHFA](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#phfa) in Pennsylvania.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**McKinney-Vento Homeless Assistance Act**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 11301 et seq.<br>
Authorizes Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG) and the Continuum of Care ([CoC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#coc)) framework. The HEARTH Act of 2009 consolidated and reauthorized homelessness assistance with the CoC architecture. The Philadelphia CoC receives federal CoC funding administered through OHS as the Collaborative Applicant.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**American Rescue Plan Act of 2021**, P.L. 117-2.<br>
Authorized 70,000-plus Emergency Housing Vouchers (EHVs) nationally; PHA received approximately 850-plus EHVs serving homeless and at-risk applicants. EHV is a one-time allocation that closes once turned over absent congressional replenishment.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Internal Revenue Code § 42 — Low-Income Housing Tax Credit** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/42)).<br>
Authorizes [LIHTC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#lihtc), administered federally by Treasury / IRS and state-allocated by PHFA in Pennsylvania. The principal federal vehicle for affordable-housing production financing.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Rehabilitation Act of 1973**, 29 U.S.C. §§ 701 et seq.<br>
Authorizes federal Vocational Rehabilitation funding to state VR agencies. In Pennsylvania, [OVR](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ovr) sits within the PA Department of Labor and Industry. Section 504 prohibits disability discrimination in federally-funded programs.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Ticket to Work and Work Incentives Improvement Act of 1999**, P.L. 106-170.<br>
Authorizes Ticket to Work and Medicaid Buy-In options. Section 1619(a) and (b) provisions of the Social Security Act preserve SSI cash and Medicaid coverage for working SSI recipients within thresholds. Plans to Achieve Self-Support ([PASS](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pass)) permit setting aside earnings for an employment-related goal without the set-aside counting against income or resource limits.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Achieving a Better Life Experience ([ABLE](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#able)) Act of 2014**, P.L. 113-295.<br>
Authorizes tax-advantaged ABLE savings accounts for individuals with disabilities. The disability-onset threshold under the ABLE Age Adjustment Act took effect as scheduled on January 1, 2026, rising from 26 to 46 nationwide — approximately 6 million additional people newly eligible.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Older Americans Act of 1965 (OAA)**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 3001 et seq.<br>
Authorizes the Administration for Community Living's aging programs. **Title III** authorizes home-and-community-based supportive services, congregate meals, home-delivered meals, preventive health, and the National Family Caregiver Support Program. **Title V** authorizes SCSEP. **Title VII** authorizes the long-term care ombudsman program and elder-abuse prevention.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Low Income Home Energy Assistance Act**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 8621 et seq.<br>
Authorizes [LIHEAP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#liheap). PA LIHEAP includes elder-priority eligibility and the Heating and Cooling Standard Utility Allowance retention for households with a member age 60 and older under OBBBA — meaning elder-headed households retain the SUA simplification removed for non-elder non-disabled households.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/nutrition-assistance/">Nutrition Assistance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Child Care and Development Block Grant (CCDBG) / Child Care and Development Fund (CCDF)**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 9858 et seq.<br>
Authorizes federal subsidized child care for low-income working families. The 2014 CCDBG Act Reauthorization established quality-rating-and-improvement requirements and 12-month minimum eligibility periods. PA's CCDF eligibility threshold is set at 200% FPL initial eligibility with a 235% FPL exit threshold.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Head Start Act**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 9831 et seq.<br>
Authorizes federally-administered Head Start and Early Head Start serving low-income pre-K children.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Child Abuse Prevention and Treatment Act (CAPTA)**, 42 U.S.C. §§ 5101 et seq.<br>
Authorizes federal funding for state child-abuse prevention and treatment programs and establishes federal floor requirements for state CPS systems.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Family First Prevention Services Act (FFPSA)**, P.L. 115-123 (2018).<br>
Amended Title IV-E to permit federal reimbursement for evidence-based prevention services to families at risk of foster-care entry; restructured residential treatment-program eligibility under the Qualified Residential Treatment Program (QRTP) framework; limited IV-E reimbursement for non-QRTP congregate care. The most significant federal child welfare reform of the past two decades.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA)**, 25 U.S.C. §§ 1901 et seq.<br>
Establishes placement preferences and procedural protections for Native American children in child welfare cases.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Social Security Fairness Act of 2025**.<br>
Repealed both the Windfall Elimination Provision and the Government Pension Offset effective for benefits payable after January 2024 — increasing benefits for affected retirees with prior public-sector employment. SSA implementation is substantially complete: more than 3.1 million WEP / GPO retroactive payments totaling approximately $17 billion processed by July 7, 2025 ("five months ahead of schedule" per SSA blog); approximately 92% of post-enactment applications completed by July 21, 2025.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

### Federal regulations

**42 C.F.R. Part 438 — Medicaid managed care**.<br>
Establishes [MCO](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#mco) contracting standards, network adequacy floors, enrollee protections, and parity requirements for Medicaid MCOs. Operative for HealthChoices oversight in Pennsylvania.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

**20 C.F.R. §§ 404.1520 and 416.920 — Five-step sequential evaluation process for disability**.<br>
SSA's process for determining disability eligibility: substantial gainful activity; severe medically determinable impairment; meets or equals a Listing of Impairments; capacity to perform past relevant work; capacity to perform other work given residual functional capacity, age, education, and work experience. Administered through state DDS agencies — in Pennsylvania, the PA Bureau of Disability Determination within PA Department of Labor and Industry.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

## State statutory layer

**Pennsylvania Human Services Code**, 62 P.S. §§ 101 et seq.<br>
Establishes DHS and program authorities for cash assistance (Article IV: 62 P.S. §§ 401–499), Medical Assistance, and other social-welfare programs. Implementing regulations at 55 Pa. Code Chapters 105–183.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

**Act 35 of 1996** — Pennsylvania's PRWORA implementation.<br>
60-month TANF lifetime limit (1,830-day clock from March 3, 1997); 24-month work-requirement clock (732 days); Agreement of Mutual Responsibility framework; 50% earned-income disregard for ongoing recipients.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

**Act 80 of 2012 / Act 12 of 2019 — General Assistance**.<br>
Act 80 of 2012 eliminated General Assistance; Act 12 of 2019 restored it after the PA Supreme Court declared the 2012 elimination procedurally invalid.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

**Act 80 of 2019** and successor budget acts.<br>
Authorize annual Medical Assistance appropriations and provider-tax components.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a>.

**Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit (WPTC)** — enacted in the 2025-26 PA budget.<br>
Refundable state credit equal to 10% of the federal EITC; maximum $805; effective tax year 2025 (filed 2026 season); estimated 940,000 Pennsylvanians eligible; approximately $193 million annual cost. The credit is automatically awarded to PA-40 filers claiming federal EITC. Pennsylvania's first state-level EITC analog.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a>.

**Pennsylvania Mental Health and Intellectual Disability Act of 1966**, 50 P.S. § 4101 et seq.<br>
Authorizes state IDD services. The PA Human Services Code further authorizes state administration of Medicaid HCBS waivers including the IDD-specific [ODP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#odp) waivers (Consolidated, Community Living, Person/Family Directed Support, Adult Autism).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Pennsylvania Older Adults Protective Services Act**, 35 P.S. §§ 10225.101 et seq.<br>
Authorizes Pennsylvania's Adult Protective Services framework for older adults.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Pennsylvania Lottery and Senior Citizens Property Tax and Rent Rebate Act**.<br>
Establishes the PTRR program funded primarily by the PA Lottery — a state-distinctive funding architecture. **Act 7 of 2023** expanded the PTRR program: increased the income limit and the maximum rebate amount for the elderly and certain disabled adults.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency Act**, 35 P.S. §§ 1680.101 et seq.<br>
Authorizes PHFA — the state administrator for LIHTC allocation in Pennsylvania, HOME state-allocation, the PA-administered portion of EHV, and PHARE (the PA Housing Affordability and Rehabilitation Enhancement Fund, state-financed).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Pennsylvania Child Protective Services Law (CPSL)**, 23 Pa.C.S. §§ 6301 et seq.<br>
Establishes Pennsylvania's child-abuse reporting, investigation, and CPS framework.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Pennsylvania Juvenile Act**, 42 Pa.C.S. §§ 6301 et seq.<br>
Establishes dependency proceedings.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Pennsylvania Adoption Act**, 23 Pa.C.S. §§ 2101 et seq.<br>
Governs adoption.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

## Local statutory layer

**Philadelphia Home Rule Charter**.<br>
The charter establishes the City of Philadelphia's governance framework including departmental structure for Philadelphia DHS as the county Children and Youth Agency, the Office of Homeless Services, the Department of Behavioral Health and Intellectual disAbility Services, the Philadelphia Department of Revenue, and the Philadelphia Housing Development Corporation.<br>
*Cited in:* every D12 sub-domain at the local administrative layer.

**Philadelphia Housing Authority (PHA) enabling architecture**.<br>
PHA operates as the city's public housing authority administering HCV, PBV, Public Housing, and PHA-administered PBS8 contracts under HUD program rules and the federal Housing Act of 1937. PHA serves approximately 19,500 HCV households and operates a combined portfolio of approximately 37,350 total assisted units.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Philadelphia Department of Behavioral Health and Intellectual disAbility Services ([DBHIDS](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#dbhids))**.<br>
Operates as a county-level intellectual-disability-and-autism Administrative Entity for ODP services. **Community Behavioral Health ([CBH](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cbh))** — Philadelphia's county-managed behavioral-health managed-care entity — operates as a non-profit county subsidiary providing every Philadelphia HealthChoices enrollee's behavioral-health benefit regardless of which physical-health MCO covers their physical care. The CBH single-MCO architecture is structurally distinctive among Pennsylvania counties.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/disability-support/">Disability Support</a>.

**Philadelphia Corporation for Aging ([PCA](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#pca))**.<br>
The Area Agency on Aging for Philadelphia, responsible for OAA Title III administration including senior-center funding, home-delivered meals coordination, family caregiver support, OPTIONS in-home services for low-income elders not Medicaid-eligible, Adult Protective Services for older adults, and SHIP ([APPRISE](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#apprise) in PA) Medicare counseling. PCA Helpline: 215-765-9040.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/elder-support/">Elder Support</a>.

**Philadelphia Office of Homeless Services (OHS)**.<br>
Administers McKinney-Vento ESG and serves as the Collaborative Applicant for the Philadelphia Continuum of Care. OHS also manages EHV referrals to PHA and operates the homelessness intake architecture (Prevention, Diversion &amp; Intake Unit; Homelessness Prevention Hotline 215-686-7177).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**Philadelphia Housing Development Corporation ([PHDC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#phdc))**.<br>
Administers PHLHousing+, a 300-household direct-cash-assistance pilot for low-AMI families on the PHA waitlist with children 15 and under (50% AMI threshold; 2.5-year duration; partner-administered with Penn Housing Initiative and the City CEO Office).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/housing-assistance/">Housing Assistance</a>.

**PHLpreK**.<br>
The city-funded pre-K program operating alongside the federal and state pre-K programs (Head Start, Pre-K Counts). PHLpreK's funding architecture and its relation to the federal-state pre-K layer is engaged at the early-childhood interface that intersects D12 Child &amp; Family Support and (cross-domain) Education.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/child-family-support/">Child &amp; Family Support</a>.

**Philadelphia County Assistance Offices ([CAOs](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cao))**.<br>
Receive applications, perform eligibility determinations, and process redeterminations for PA DHS-administered programs (TANF, GA, SNAP eligibility, Medicaid eligibility) through PA's CAO network operating across Philadelphia. The Philadelphia CAOs handle Philadelphia-County applicants under PA DHS oversight.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/income-maintenance/">Income Maintenance</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/medicaid-coverage/">Medicaid &amp; Health Coverage</a> · <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/social-welfare/sub-domains/nutrition-assistance/">Nutrition Assistance</a>.
