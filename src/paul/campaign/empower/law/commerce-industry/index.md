# Legal text appendix — Commerce &amp; Industry

<p class="domain-lead">The legal chain that produces PA-3's Commerce &amp; Industry architecture, organized constitutional → federal → state → local. The federal substantive layer threads through <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ecoa">ECOA</a>, Dodd-Frank § 1071, Small Business Act with the 7(a) / 504 / Microloan / <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#8a">8(a)</a> / <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hubzone">HUBZone</a> / <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#wosb">WOSB</a> / <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sbdc">SBDC</a> architecture, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ftc">FTC</a> Act § 5, FTC Franchise Rule, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cdfi">CDFI</a> Act, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cra">CRA</a>, <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nmtc">NMTC</a>, IRC §§ 1400Z-1 / 1400Z-2 Opportunity Zones; the regulatory architecture above the statutory floor is currently operating under substantial reshaping — MC01 § 1071 2026 Final Rule (May 1, 2026) narrowing scope with January 2028 compliance; MC02 <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sba">SBA</a> 8(a) race-neutral restructuring (January 22, 2026; ~65 admissions in 2025; 1,000+ participants suspended); MC04 OBBBA <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#qoz">QOZ</a> permanent (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025); MC05 OBBBA NMTC permanent; MC06 CRA 2023 Final Rule rescission NPR (July 16, 2025); MC07 CDFI Fund FY 2026 $324M level funding with ~$298M FY 2025 frozen by <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#omb">OMB</a>. The state layer threads through PA Constitution Art. III §§ 11, 24; PA Business Corporation Law at 15 Pa.C.S. § 1101 et seq.; PA LLC Act at 15 Pa.C.S. § 8901 et seq. with MC08 Act 122 of 2022 effective January 1, 2025; PA <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#utpcpl">UTPCPL</a> at 73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq.; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#hicpa">HICPA</a> at 73 P.S. § 517.1 et seq.; Pa. R. Civ. P. 2950 confession-of-judgment procedure; Keystone Opportunity Zone Act at 73 P.S. § 820.101 et seq.; PA <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#bid">BID</a> enabling at 53 P.S. § 18101 et seq. The Philadelphia layer threads through Philadelphia Code Title 9 (Business Privilege License; § 9-203 general vendor licensing; Title 9 food business licensing); Philadelphia Code Ch. 17-1600 (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#oeo">OEO</a> <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#mbe">MBE</a>/<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#wbe">WBE</a>/<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#dsbe">DSBE</a> certification); Philadelphia Code § 19-2600 (<a href="/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#birt">BIRT</a>); Philadelphia Code Ch. 19-1600 (BIDs); MC03 OEO Disparity Study FY 2021 most recent (January 25, 2023). For the analytical treatment of how each instrument operates and where its gaps fall, see the seven <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/">D8 sub-domain pages</a>.</p>

## Constitutional foundation

### U.S. Constitution

**Article I § 8 — Commerce Clause, Taxing Clause, and Spending Clause** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/articlei#section8)).<br>
Commerce Clause supplies federal authority for ECOA and the Small Business Act; Taxing Clause grounds the IRC tax-credit instruments (QOZ, NMTC); Spending Clause is the basis for SBA grant programs (SBDC, Microloan intermediaries, WBC) and CDFI Fund discretionary awards.<br>
*Cited in:* every D8 sub-domain.

**Fifth Amendment** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/fifth_amendment)).<br>
Due Process / equal protection component grounds the strict-scrutiny analysis of federal race-conscious set-aside programs under <em>Adarand</em>. <em>Ultima Services</em> applied this framework to the SBA's 8(a) racial presumption, producing the constitutional predicate for MC02.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>.

**14th Amendment** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentxiv)).<br>
Equal Protection Clause grounds the strict-scrutiny analysis of state and local race-conscious set-aside programs under <em>Croson</em>; the constitutional predicate for Philadelphia's OEO MBE/WBE program's disparity-study requirement.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/procurement-mbe-wbe/">Procurement, MBE/WBE &amp; Anchor Institution Economic Integration</a>.

### Key foundational cases

***City of Richmond v. J.A. Croson Co.***, **488 U.S. 469 (1989)** ([Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/488/469/)).<br>
State and local race-conscious set-aside programs subject to strict scrutiny; require compelling government interest (typically demonstrated by methodologically sound disparity study) and narrow tailoring. Constitutional predicate for the MC03 disparity-study-currency concern.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/procurement-mbe-wbe/">Procurement, MBE/WBE &amp; Anchor Institution Economic Integration</a>.

***Adarand Constructors v. Peña***, **515 U.S. 200 (1995)** ([Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/515/200/)).<br>
Extends strict-scrutiny analysis to federal race-conscious set-aside programs. Constitutional foundation for the constraint that 8(a)'s racial presumption of social disadvantage could not survive.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>.

***Ultima Services Corp. v. USDA***, **No. 2:20-cv-00041 (E.D. Tenn. 2023)**.<br>
Held that the SBA 8(a) program's racial presumption of social disadvantage was unconstitutional as applied; enjoined SBA from applying the racial presumption. Predicate event for MC02 — SBA's January 22, 2026 formal race-neutral guidance.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>.

***Texas Bankers Association v. CFPB***, **No. 7:23-cv-00144 (S.D. Tex. 2023)**.<br>
Primary litigation vehicle staying the 2023 § 1071 Final Rule (88 Fed. Reg. 35150). Litigation produced the regulatory posture culminating in MC01 — the CFPB's substantially narrowed 2026 Final Rule.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>.

### Pennsylvania Constitution

**PA Constitution Article III §§ 11, 24** (PA Const. art. III §§ 11, 24).<br>
Authorize the General Assembly to enact business-regulation, consumer-protection, and commercial-licensing legislation. Constitutional foundation for the PA UTPCPL, HICPA, the PA Business Corporation Law, the PA LLC Act, and the KOZ Act.<br>
*Cited in:* every D8 sub-domain.

## Federal statutes — credit access &amp; data infrastructure

**Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)** ([15 U.S.C. § 1691 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-41/subchapter-IV)).<br>
Enacted 1974; amended 1976 to add small business credit coverage. Prohibits discrimination in any aspect of a credit transaction (including small business credit) on the basis of race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, receipt of public assistance, or good-faith exercise of rights under the Consumer Credit Protection Act. Requires creditors to provide written adverse-action notice when credit is denied. Enforcement: [CFPB](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#cfpb) (primary federal); [DOJ](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#doj) for pattern-or-practice referrals. Private right of action with actual and punitive damages; class-action certification available. <strong>Structural enforcement gap (G8-SD1-04)</strong>: discouraged borrowers who do not apply because they expect denial never generate an adverse-action record, never trigger the complaint-driven enforcement architecture; [SBCS](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#sbcs) 2022 documents ~37% Black-owned discouragement rate vs. ~14% white-owned.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>.

**Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act § 1071** ([15 U.S.C. § 1691c-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1691c-2)).<br>
Amends ECOA to require covered financial institutions to collect and report data on small business credit applications and originations, disaggregated by race, ethnicity, sex, and gross annual revenue of the applicant. <strong>MC01 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — § 1071 2026 Final Rule</strong> (published May 1, 2026) supersedes the 2023 rule and its compliance-date extensions. The 2026 Final Rule narrows § 1071 coverage materially: origination threshold 100 → 1,000; [MCAs](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#mca), agricultural lending, and loans under $1,000 expressly excluded; small business revenue definition $5M → $1M in gross annual revenue; single compliance date January 1, 2028. <em>Rise Economy v. Vought</em> in D.C. district court is the primary vehicle for civil-rights-advocate challenges favoring the broader 2023 framework.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a> (principal anchor); <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a> (secondary anchor).

## Federal statutes — small business programs and set-asides

**Small Business Act** ([15 U.S.C. § 631 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-14A)).<br>
Authorizes the U.S. Small Business Administration and the federal small business program architecture. <strong>7(a) loan program</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 636(a): SBA-guaranteed loans up to $5 million; primary general-purpose SBA lending instrument. <strong>504 loan program</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 696: long-term fixed-rate financing for real estate and equipment through Certified Development Companies. <strong>Microloan program</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 636(m): loans up to $50,000 (national average disbursement ~$13,000) through nonprofit intermediary lenders; bundled with required technical assistance. <strong>8(a) Business Development</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 637(a): nine-year program providing federal contracting set-asides for small businesses owned by socially and economically disadvantaged individuals. <strong>MC02 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — SBA formal guidance January 22, 2026</strong>: 8(a) operative as race-neutral; racial presumption permanently eliminated post-<em>Ultima Services</em>; ~65 admissions in 2025 vs. hundreds historically; 1,000+ existing participants suspended in January 2026. <strong>HUBZone</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 657a: contracting set-asides for small businesses located in and employing residents of Historically Underutilized Business Zones. <strong>WOSB (Women-Owned Small Business)</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 637(m): set-asides in industries where women are underrepresented in federal contracting. <strong>SBDC</strong> at 15 U.S.C. § 648: Small Business Development Centers providing technical assistance through university and nonprofit hosts.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a> (principal); <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a> (Microloan secondary).

**Small Business Jobs Act of 2010** (P.L. 111-240).<br>
Post-recession legislative enhancement; increased 7(a) and 504 loan limits; temporary fee elimination; expanded Export Express. Documents the legislative pattern of emergency-era enhancement followed by return to baseline conditions — temporally relevant to PPP exit.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>.

**Coronavirus Aid, Relief, and Economic Security (CARES) Act, P.L. 116-136 — Paycheck Protection Program ([PPP](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ppp))** (March 27, 2020; subsequent appropriations).<br>
Deployed SBA-backed forgivable loans through existing 7(a) lender networks at emergency scale. Documented racial-gap natural experiment: predominantly Black ZIP codes received fewer early loans; Black-owned businesses received smaller amounts and were pushed toward fintech channels carrying higher fees; Fairlie ([NBER](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#nber) WP 27462) documented 41% decline in Black business ownership in early COVID-19 months. CDFI PPP performance differential: CDFI-originated PPP loans reached higher share of Black-owned businesses than bank-originated loans; CDFI scale insufficient to close the access gap.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/federal-set-asides/">Federal Small Business Programs &amp; Set-Asides</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>.

## Federal statutes — consumer protection &amp; commercial practices

**Federal Trade Commission Act § 5** ([15 U.S.C. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45)).<br>
Prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in commerce. Enforcement: FTC administrative; FTC litigation; no private right of action under § 5. The federal consumer-protection floor on which PA's UTPCPL operates as the state-level private-right-of-action complement.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/consumer-protection/">Consumer Protection &amp; Predatory Commercial Practices</a>.

**FTC Franchise Rule** ([16 C.F.R. § 436](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-436)).<br>
Requires franchisors to provide prospective franchisees with a Franchise Disclosure Document ([FDD](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#fdd)) before sale. The FDD must include 23 specified items including financial performance representations. PA UTPCPL deceptive-practice claims under 73 P.S. § 201-2(4)(xxi) are the operative private right of action for franchisee misrepresentation harm; no FTC Act private right of action.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/consumer-protection/">Consumer Protection &amp; Predatory Commercial Practices</a>.

## Federal statutes — CDFI, CRA, and place-based investment

**Riegle Community Development and Regulatory Improvement Act of 1994 / CDFI Act** ([12 U.S.C. § 4701 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/chapter-47)).<br>
Established the Community Development Financial Institutions Fund within the U.S. Department of the Treasury. Authorizes CDFI certification, the CDFI Program (Financial Assistance and Technical Assistance grants), Bank Enterprise Award Program, Native Initiatives, Healthy Food Financing Initiative, and the Capital Magnet Fund. <strong>MC07 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — FY 2026 Consolidated Appropriations Act (signed February 3, 2026)</strong>: CDFI Fund received $324 million (level funding); President's initial FY 2026 budget proposed $133 million; Congress restored level funding; [NCRC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ncrc) reported ~$298 million of FY 2025 appropriations remained unreleased by OMB as of February 2026.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>.

**Community Reinvestment Act (CRA)** ([12 U.S.C. § 2901 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/12/chapter-30)).<br>
Enacted 1977. Requires federally regulated depository institutions to meet the credit needs of their assessment areas, including [LMI](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#lmi) neighborhoods. CRA examination criteria include small business lending assessment. Data accessible through [FFIEC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#ffiec), [OCC](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#occ), and Federal Reserve. <strong>MC06 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — 2023 Final Rule rescission</strong>: 2023 Final Rule (88 Fed. Reg. 228) stayed by preliminary injunction March 29, 2024 (N.D. Texas) and never took effect; OCC/Fed/FDIC announced rescission March 28, 2025; joint NPR to formally rescind issued July 16, 2025; 1995 CRA framework continues to govern all banks. The expanded assessment area, LMI evaluation criteria, and small business lending accountability mechanisms of the 2023 rule are not operative.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/cdfi-lending/">CDFI Lending &amp; the Small Business Credit Market</a>.

**Community Renewal Tax Relief Act of 2000 / OBBBA of 2025 — New Markets Tax Credit (NMTC)** ([26 U.S.C. § 45D](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/45D); statutory authority at 12 U.S.C. § 4701 et seq.).<br>
39% federal tax credit spread over 7 years for Qualified Equity Investments in Community Development Entities deploying capital in Low-Income Communities (census tracts with ≥20% poverty rate or median income ≤80% AMI). CDFI Fund allocates NMTC authority to CDEs competitively; typical annual allocation ~$5 billion. <strong>MC05 — OBBBA P.L. 119-21 (July 4, 2025) made NMTC permanent</strong> (indefinite statutory authorization). FY 2026 Consolidated Appropriations Act (February 3, 2026) maintains level funding.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/economic-development-zones/">Economic Development Zones &amp; Place-Based Investment Tools</a>.

**Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 / OBBBA of 2025 — Qualified Opportunity Zones** ([IRC §§ 1400Z-1, 1400Z-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1400Z-1)).<br>
Investors roll existing capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund (QOF) within 180 days of realization; gains are deferred; appreciation on the QOF investment held ≥10 years is excluded from capital gains. Final regulations: T.D. 9889 (Dec. 2019), 84 Fed. Reg. 69442. <strong>MC04 PRINCIPAL ANCHOR — OBBBA P.L. 119-21 (July 4, 2025) made QOZ program permanent</strong>: new designation round effective January 1, 2027 (10-year designation period); governors designate new zones by July 1, 2026; CDFI Fund public comment on the new nomination tool with comments due May 5, 2026. Original TCJA designation round capital-gains-deferral benefit (December 31, 2026 deadline) still applies to existing investments made before that date; post-2026 investments operate under a revised incentive structure (5-year deferral; 10% step-up in basis for non-rural zones).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/economic-development-zones/">Economic Development Zones &amp; Place-Based Investment Tools</a>.

## Pennsylvania statutes — business formation &amp; LLC reporting

**Pennsylvania Business Corporation Law** ([15 Pa.C.S. § 1101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=15)).<br>
Governs business corporation formation and governance in Pennsylvania.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>.

**Pennsylvania Limited Liability Company Act** ([15 Pa.C.S. § 8901 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=15)).<br>
Governs LLC formation and governance; the primary entity form for PA-3 small business formation. Formation: articles of organization ($125 PA DOS filing fee); registered agent; operating agreement. <strong>MC08 — Act 122 of 2022 (effective January 1, 2025)</strong>: replaced Pennsylvania's decennial report system with a new annual reporting requirement; LLCs must file Annual Report (DSCB:15-146) by September 30 annually; $7 annual fee for LLCs. First reports were required by September 30, 2025 for entities formed before 2025; full enforcement begins with 2027 reports.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>.

## Pennsylvania statutes — consumer protection

**Pennsylvania Unfair Trade Practices and Consumer Protection Law (UTPCPL)** ([73 P.S. § 201-1 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/CFDOCS/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=73&amp;div=0&amp;chpt=83)).<br>
Prohibits unfair methods of competition and unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce. § 201-2(4) enumerates twenty-one specified categories of deceptive practice including the catch-all xxi: "engaging in any other fraudulent or deceptive conduct which creates a likelihood of confusion or of misunderstanding." § 201-9.2 provides private right of action with mandatory <strong>treble damages</strong> and attorney's fees for private enforcement. PA AG and District Attorney enforcement authority for public action. <strong>UTPCPL coverage is limited for commercial transactions</strong> — a structural feature relevant to the MCA regulatory no-man's-land at G8-SD5-02.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/consumer-protection/">Consumer Protection &amp; Predatory Commercial Practices</a>.

**Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act (HICPA)** ([73 P.S. § 517.1 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=73&amp;div=0&amp;chpt=110)).<br>
Effective July 1, 2009. Requires PA AG contractor registration for home improvement contractors performing $500+ work. Mandatory contract terms; payment-schedule rules (deposit limits); right of rescission. UTPCPL deceptive-practice claims available for HICPA violations. <strong>HICPA's registration requirement and payment schedule rules protect consumers transacting with registered contractors; the deregistered or never-registered contractor operates entirely outside the protection framework</strong> — G8-SD5-01 protection-thinnest-where-harm-greatest finding.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/consumer-protection/">Consumer Protection &amp; Predatory Commercial Practices</a>.

**Pennsylvania Rule of Civil Procedure 2950 — Confession of Judgment** ([Pa. R. Civ. P. 2950](https://www.pacourts.us/assets/opinions/Supreme/out/231-RAP-2018mo.pdf)).<br>
Permits confession of judgment for money based on a warrant of attorney contained in a written instrument signed by the person to be bound. The MCA debt-collection mechanism: an MCA contract includes a COJ clause authorizing the lender to obtain judgment in PA state court upon default, without court proceeding. <strong>The COJ mechanism, combined with MCA products falling outside ECOA, UTPCPL commercial coverage, and PA usury law, produces the regulatory no-man's-land documented at G8-SD5-02.</strong><br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/consumer-protection/">Consumer Protection &amp; Predatory Commercial Practices</a>.

## Pennsylvania statutes — place-based investment &amp; BIDs

**Keystone Opportunity Zone Act** ([73 P.S. § 820.101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=73&amp;div=0&amp;chpt=63)).<br>
PA-state-administered [KOZ](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#koz) program. Designated tracts receive state-level tax abatements (Corporate Net Income Tax; Capital Stock/Foreign Franchise Tax; Bank Shares Tax; certain Use Tax; certain local taxes). Administered by PA [DCED](/paul/campaign/empower/glossary/#dced). KOZ utilization gap finding (G8-SD4-02) addresses the question of program reach within PA-3 designated tracts.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/economic-development-zones/">Economic Development Zones &amp; Place-Based Investment Tools</a>.

**Pennsylvania Business Improvement District enabling legislation** ([53 P.S. § 18101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/uconsCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;yr=2000&amp;sessInd=0&amp;smthLwInd=0&amp;act=130)).<br>
Authorizes municipalities to establish Business Improvement Districts (BIDs) and Neighborhood Improvement Districts (NIDs) funded through property-owner assessments. BID resource asymmetry finding (G8-SD4-03 Both/And) — BIDs create substantive corridor value AND BID resource inequality reproduces commercial corridor disadvantage between gentrifying and disinvested corridors.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/economic-development-zones/">Economic Development Zones &amp; Place-Based Investment Tools</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/commercial-corridors/">Commercial Corridor Vitality, Vacancy &amp; the Informal Economy</a>.

## Philadelphia ordinances — business licensing, taxation &amp; certification

**Philadelphia Code Title 9 — Business Privilege License (BPL)** ([Phila. Code Title 9](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/philadelphia/latest/philadelphia_pa/0-0-0-1)).<br>
Annual business operating license. Required for all businesses operating in Philadelphia. Administered by Department of Licenses &amp; Inspections.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/commercial-corridors/">Commercial Corridor Vitality, Vacancy &amp; the Informal Economy</a>.

**Philadelphia Business Income and Receipts Tax (BIRT)** ([Phila. Code § 19-2600](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/philadelphia/latest/philadelphia_pa/0-0-0-194568)).<br>
Combined gross-receipts (~0.1415%) and net-income (~5.81%) tax on business activity in Philadelphia. The gross-receipts component applies regardless of profitability and is a documented compliance burden on low-margin and early-stage businesses. Cross-reference D9 SD3 for the fiscal-side treatment.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/small-business-capital/">Small Business Formation &amp; Access to Capital</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/commerce-industry/sub-domains/commercial-corridors/">Commercial Corridor Vitality, Vacancy &amp; the Informal Economy</a>.

**Philadelphia Code Chapter 17-1600 — Office of Economic Opportunity / MBE/WBE/DSBE certification** ([Phila. Code Ch. 17-1600](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/philadelphia/latest/philadelphia_pa/0-0-0-209487)).<br>
Administered by the Department of Commerce / Office of Economic Opportunity. Provides certification as Minority Business Enterprise (MBE), Women Business Enterprise (WBE), and Disabled-Owned Business Enterprise (DSBE) for purposes of city contract participation. <strong>MC03 — OEO Disparity Study FY 2021</strong> (January 25, 2023, Econsult Solutions consultant) is the most recent disparity study; no FY 2022-2024 study published as of May 2026. <em>Croson</em> constitutional-currency concern is confirmed live at G8-SD3-04.<br>
