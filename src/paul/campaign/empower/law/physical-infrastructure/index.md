# Legal text appendix — Physical Infrastructure

<p class="domain-lead">The legal chain that produces PA-3's Physical Infrastructure architecture, organized constitutional → federal → state → local. The federal substantive layer threads through Chapter 53 of Title 49 U.S.C. (FTA Sections 5307 / 5337 / 5339 / 5310 / 5309), 23 U.S.C. § 101 et seq. (federal-aid highway program), ADA Title II (42 U.S.C. § 12131 et seq.), Title VI (42 U.S.C. § 2000d), Safe Drinking Water Act (42 U.S.C. §§ 300f-300j-27), Clean Water Act (33 U.S.C. §§ 1251 et seq.), RCRA (42 U.S.C. §§ 6901 et seq.), CERCLA (42 U.S.C. §§ 9601 et seq.), AHERA (15 U.S.C. §§ 2641-2656), Land and Water Conservation Fund Act (54 U.S.C. §§ 200301 et seq.), IIJA (P.L. 117-58), OBBBA (P.L. 119-21), and P.L. 119-75 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026). The state layer threads through PA Const. art. I § 27 (Environmental Rights Amendment); PA Const. art. III § 14 ("Public School System" clause); PA Const. art. VIII § 11 (Motor License Fund constitutional dedication); Act 89 of 2013; PA Public School Code (24 P.S. §§ 1-101 et seq.); PA Safe Drinking Water Act (35 P.S. §§ 721.1 et seq.); PA Clean Streams Law (35 P.S. §§ 691.1 et seq.); PA Solid Waste Management Act (35 P.S. §§ 6018.101 et seq.); PA Hazardous Sites Cleanup Act (35 P.S. §§ 6020.101 et seq.); PA Vehicle Code (75 Pa.C.S. §§ 101 et seq.). The Philadelphia layer threads through the Philadelphia Home Rule Charter; Philadelphia Code Title 5 (Parks and Recreation); Title 9 (Transportation); Title 10 (Regulation of Individual Conduct and Activity); Title 11 (Education); Title 15 (City-Owned Property — Recreation); Title 21 (Public Places). For the analytical treatment of how each instrument operates and where its gaps fall, see the seven <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/">D13 sub-domain pages</a>.</p>

## Constitutional foundation

### U.S. Constitution

**Article I § 8 — Commerce Clause, Spending Clause, and Taxing Clause** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/articlei#section8)).<br>
Commerce Clause supplies federal authority for transit (Ch. 53 of Title 49), federal-aid highway program (23 U.S.C. § 101 et seq.), CWA / SDWA / RCRA / CERCLA / AHERA — each premised on the regulation of infrastructure activity affecting interstate commerce. Spending Clause is the basis for conditional federal grants to states (IIJA, DWSRF/CWSRF, LWCF, CDBG, ORLP, federal-aid highway pass-through). Taxing Clause grounds federal tax-credit instruments where applicable.<br>
*Cited in:* every D13 sub-domain.

**Article IV § 3 cl. 2 — Property Clause** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/articleiv#section3)).<br>
Grounds federal management of federal lands (NPS National Park System units; FWS National Wildlife Refuge System including Heinz NWR at Tinicum).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a>.

**Fourteenth Amendment Equal Protection Clause** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentxiv)).<br>
Grounds Title VI applied to federally-funded infrastructure programs (transit Title VI service equity; federal-aid highway disparate-impact analysis; SDWA / CWA Title VI EJ overlay).<br>
*Cited in:* SD1, SD2, SD3, SD4, SD6, SD7.

**Tenth Amendment** ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/constitution/amendmentx)).<br>
Establishes the federalism framework within which federal-state-local infrastructure architecture operates; state primacy delegations under SDWA, CWA, RCRA Subtitle D depend on this framework.<br>
*Cited in:* SD2, SD4, SD7.

### Pennsylvania Constitution

**PA Constitution Article I § 27 — Environmental Rights Amendment** (PA Const. art. I § 27).<br>
Ratified May 18, 1971. Provides three operative components: the people's right to clean air, pure water, and preservation of natural, scenic, historic, and esthetic values; common-property status of public natural resources; the Commonwealth as trustee. Reinvigorated by <em>Robinson Township, Delaware Riverkeeper Network, et al. v. Commonwealth</em>, 83 A.3d 901 (Pa. 2013) as a substantive constitutional limit on state action affecting environmental rights.<br>
*Cited in:* SD2 (water resources); SD4 (solid waste); SD6 (parkland and open-space conservation).

**PA Constitution Article III § 14 — "Public School System" clause** (PA Const. art. III § 14).<br>
"The General Assembly shall provide for the maintenance and support of a thorough and efficient system of public education to serve the needs of the Commonwealth." Substantive constitutional foundation underlying <em>William Penn School District v. Commonwealth</em> education funding adequacy litigation.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/schools/">School Buildings &amp; Public Facility Capital</a>.

**PA Constitution Article VIII § 11 — Motor License Fund** (PA Const. art. VIII § 11).<br>
Constitutionally dedicates fuel-tax and vehicle-license revenue to highway purposes. Unlike the Act 89 transit architecture's General Fund route, the Motor License Fund's constitutional dedication is robust.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/roads/">Roads, Bridges &amp; Pedestrian Infrastructure</a>.

### Key foundational cases

***William Penn School District v. Commonwealth***, **170 A.3d 414 (Pa. 2017)** (justiciability); **282 A.3d 1247 (Pa. 2022)**; **297 A.3d 1163 (Pa. Commw. Ct. 2023)**.<br>
PA Supreme Court 2017 decision established justiciability of the "thorough and efficient" clause; 2023 Commonwealth Court decision found PA's school funding system unconstitutional and ordered legislative remediation. MC-05 William Penn $565M legislative response (PA budget November 12, 2025) operates against an Education Law Center December 2025 estimated ~$3.8B remaining adequacy gap.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/schools/">School Buildings &amp; Public Facility Capital</a>.

***Robinson Township v. Commonwealth***, **83 A.3d 901 (Pa. 2013)**.<br>
PA Supreme Court decision reinvigorating Article I § 27 as substantive constitutional limit on state action affecting environmental rights after decades of <em>Payne v. Kassab</em> balancing-test dormancy.<br>
*Cited in:* SD2, SD4, SD6.

## Federal statutes — transit and surface transportation

**Federal Public Transportation Program** ([Chapter 53 of Title 49, U.S.C.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/subtitle-III/chapter-53)).<br>
Authorizes FTA Section 5307 (Urbanized Area Formula Program); Section 5337 (State of Good Repair Formula Program); Section 5339 (Bus and Bus Facilities); Section 5310 (Enhanced Mobility of Seniors and Individuals with Disabilities); Section 5309 (Capital Investment Grants — New Starts / Small Starts / Core Capacity). Section 5307 operates as statutory entitlement; CIG operates with significant administrative discretion at every stage.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/transit/">Transit &amp; Mobility</a>.

**Federal-Aid Highway Program** ([23 U.S.C. § 101 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/23)).<br>
Authorizes National Highway Performance Program, Surface Transportation Block Grant Program (STBG), Highway Safety Improvement Program (HSIP), Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality Improvement (CMAQ), Bridge Investment Program. **STBG Transportation Alternatives set-aside at 23 U.S.C. § 133(h)** supports non-motorized infrastructure. **Recreational Trails Program at 23 U.S.C. § 206** supports trail acquisition, construction, and maintenance.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/roads/">Roads, Bridges &amp; Pedestrian Infrastructure</a>; <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a>.

**Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA), P.L. 117-58** ([P.L. 117-58, November 15, 2021](https://www.congress.gov/117/plaws/publ58/PLAW-117publ58.pdf)).<br>
Authorized surface transportation programs FY 2022-2026 with up to $108B for federal public transportation, $43.4B for DWSRF/CWSRF, $432B total for surface transportation, plus new programs (Reconnecting Communities, SS4A, Bridge Investment). <strong>IIJA expires September 30, 2026</strong> — the proximate inflection point for all six substantive D13 sub-domains per MC-02. Cross-reference CRS R48845 / R48644 / R48728.<br>
*Cited in:* all seven sub-domains; principal anchor at SD7.

**Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75)** ([H.R. 7148, February 3, 2026](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/7148)).<br>
Signed February 3, 2026 ending the partial government shutdown. THUD Act provisions transferred ~$2.3B in unobligated IIJA balances — ~$879M from NEVI Program; Reconnecting Communities cut to $30M FY 2026 (15% of $200M obligated); CRISI rail at $7M new FY 2026 appropriations (93% reduction from FY 2025). Per MC-01.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/federal-funding/">Federal Infrastructure Funding Architecture</a> (principal); SD1, SD3.

**One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), P.L. 119-21** ([P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1)).<br>
Restructured the IRA-funded portion of the federal infrastructure-program horizon. Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund statutory authority repealed; Environmental Justice Block Grants eliminated; Climate Pollution Reduction Grants eliminated; Neighborhood Access and Equity rescinded; municipal bond tax-exempt status preserved; LIHTC +12% for 9% credits; new 125% optional fee for expedited NEPA review.<br>
*Cited in:* all seven sub-domains; principal anchor at <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/federal-funding/">SD7</a>.

## Federal statutes — civil rights and accessibility

**Americans with Disabilities Act Title II** ([42 U.S.C. § 12131 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-126/subchapter-II)).<br>
Requires accessible transit operations and complementary paratransit (49 CFR Part 37); accessible sidewalks, curb ramps, and pedestrian signals on public rights-of-way (28 CFR Part 35; U.S. Access Board PROWAG). Enforcement: DOJ Civil Rights Division; FTA accessibility-program oversight; private right of action.<br>
*Cited in:* SD1, SD3.

**Title VI of the Civil Rights Act** ([42 U.S.C. § 2000d](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/2000d)).<br>
Prohibits discrimination in federally-funded programs and activities. Applied to federally-funded transit through FTA Circular 4702.1B service-equity and fare-equity requirements. The September 4, 2025 court order reversing SEPTA service cuts operated on a Title VI disparate-impact theory (Bochetto plaintiffs Lance Haver et al., Philadelphia Court of Common Pleas; per MC-04).<br>
*Cited in:* SD1, SD3, SD7.

## Federal statutes — water infrastructure

**Safe Drinking Water Act (SDWA)** ([42 U.S.C. §§ 300f et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-6A/subchapter-XII)).<br>
Primary federal drinking-water statute. EPA promulgates National Primary Drinking Water Regulations under SDWA authority codified at 40 CFR Part 141. State primacy is the implementing mechanism; PA holds primacy. **Lead and Copper Rule architecture** in three iterations: LCR (1991; 15 ppb action level) → LCRR (2021; compliance date October 16, 2024) → **LCRI** (final rule October 30, 2024; effective December 30, 2024; compliance date November 1, 2027; 10 ppb action level; lead trigger level removed; service line replacement plans required). Cross-reference D6 MC-06 — LCRI defended by EPA in <em>AWWA v. EPA</em> with Respondents' Brief February 20, 2026.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/water/">Water &amp; Stormwater Infrastructure</a>.

**Clean Water Act (CWA)** ([33 U.S.C. §§ 1251 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/33/chapter-26)).<br>
Primary federal surface-water statute. Section 402 NPDES permit program (33 U.S.C. § 1342); Section 404 dredge-and-fill permits (33 U.S.C. § 1344; jointly administered with USACE); Clean Water State Revolving Fund (33 U.S.C. § 1383). MS4 Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System program operates as sub-authority of NPDES. PWD's 2011 Pennsylvania DEP Consent Order &amp; Agreement (per MC-11 terminology corrected from "consent decree") governs the 25-year Green City, Clean Waters Long-Term Control Plan Update through 2036.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/water/">Water &amp; Stormwater Infrastructure</a>.

## Federal statutes — solid waste, hazardous waste, and remediation

**Resource Conservation and Recovery Act (RCRA)** ([42 U.S.C. §§ 6901 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-82)).<br>
Primary federal solid waste statute. Subtitle D (§§ 6941-6949a) governs non-hazardous solid waste with minimum federal landfill standards but state-led implementation; Subtitle C (§§ 6921-6939g) governs hazardous waste with EPA-administered cradle-to-grave tracking and state primacy; Subtitle I (§§ 6991-6991m) governs underground storage tanks. <strong>RCRA Subtitle D does not address illegal dumping operationally</strong>; municipal operations are not federally funded at scale (G13-SD4-02).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/waste/">Solid Waste, Sanitation &amp; Illegal Dumping</a>.

**Comprehensive Environmental Response, Compensation, and Liability Act (CERCLA)** ([42 U.S.C. §§ 9601 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/chapter-103)).<br>
The "Superfund" statute. Authorizes EPA cleanup of hazardous substance releases; creates liability for responsible parties (PRPs). The Lower Darby Creek Area Superfund site (NPL since 2001) covers Clearview Landfill (Eastwick) and Folcroft Landfill (Delaware County) straddling the PA-3 / PA-5 boundary.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/waste/">Solid Waste, Sanitation &amp; Illegal Dumping</a>.

## Federal statutes — school buildings and parks

**Asbestos Hazard Emergency Response Act (AHERA)** ([15 U.S.C. §§ 2641-2656](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-53/subchapter-II)).<br>
Requires local education agencies to inspect school buildings for asbestos-containing materials, develop and implement asbestos management plans, conduct re-inspections every three years, and provide operations and maintenance (O&amp;M). Criminal enforcement: EPA Criminal Investigations Division (EPA-CID) partners with U.S. Attorneys' Offices. **MC-07 AHERA Deferred Prosecution Agreement** against SDP filed June 26, 2025 by U.S. Attorney David Metcalf (EDPA); 5-year monitoring period through ~2030. <strong>Cross-domain principal anchor: D6 G6-SD4-02</strong> (D6 verified file 2026-05-11; upgraded [F] LOW-MEDIUM → [D] HIGH per D6 MC-03).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/schools/">School Buildings &amp; Public Facility Capital</a>.

**Land and Water Conservation Fund Act** ([54 U.S.C. §§ 200301 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/54/200302)).<br>
Establishes LWCF for federal acquisition of federal lands (federal side) and grants to states for parks and recreation (state side), funded primarily by Outer Continental Shelf oil and gas lease revenue. Permanently authorized and funded under the **Dingell Act of 2019** and **Great American Outdoors Act (GAOA) of 2020**. LWCF FY 2026 funding protected by Congress against the Administration's 43% diversion proposal (per LWCF Coalition praise, July 22, 2025). **ORLP** (Outdoor Recreation Legacy Partnership Program) for urban areas at 50% match with priority to economically disadvantaged areas.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a>.

## Pennsylvania statutes — transit, highways, and education

**Pennsylvania Act 89 of 2013** (P.L. 974, No. 89; codified across 75 Pa.C.S. and 74 Pa.C.S.).<br>
Restructured PA transit funding architecture: dedicated the full $450M annual PA Turnpike Commission payment to public transit; eliminated the $0.12/gallon state retail gas and diesel tax; raised the cap on the Oil Company Franchise Tax. <strong>The 2022 Act 89 transition</strong> reduced PA Turnpike Commission's annual payment to PennDOT from $450M to $50M, with the $400M difference shifting to the state General Fund — the structural predicate for the 2024-2026 SEPTA fiscal crisis (G13-SD1-03).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/transit/">Transit &amp; Mobility</a>.

**Pennsylvania Vehicle Code** ([75 Pa.C.S. § 101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=75)).<br>
Governs traffic regulation and speed limits. Philadelphia currently lacks unilateral authority to set lower speed limits city-wide; Vision Zero state-legislation needs include speed-limit authority, traffic camera expansion, curb-definition reform (G13-SD3-04).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/roads/">Roads, Bridges &amp; Pedestrian Infrastructure</a>.

**Pennsylvania Public School Code of 1949** ([24 P.S. §§ 1-101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=24)).<br>
Primary state statute governing public education in Pennsylvania. Establishes school district architecture, funding mechanisms, curriculum mandates, facilities requirements. **PSFIG (Public School Facility Improvement Grant Program)** under Act 34 of 2023 amended by Act 54 of 2024 and Act 45 of 2025 — $500K-$5M grants; 25% local match; January 5 – March 13, 2026 application window. **PlanCon 2.0 (Act 70 of 2019)** authorized but unfunded.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/schools/">School Buildings &amp; Public Facility Capital</a>.

## Pennsylvania statutes — environmental

**Pennsylvania Safe Drinking Water Act** ([35 P.S. §§ 721.1 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=35)).<br>
Authorizes state SDWA primacy. PA DEP Bureau of Safe Drinking Water administers; Southeast Regional Office in Norristown serves Philadelphia County.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/water/">Water &amp; Stormwater Infrastructure</a>.

**Pennsylvania Clean Streams Law** ([35 P.S. §§ 691.1 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=35)).<br>
Authorizes state implementation of CWA-delegated programs. PWD's 2011 Consent Order &amp; Agreement (per MC-11) operates under this statute.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/water/">Water &amp; Stormwater Infrastructure</a>.

**Pennsylvania Solid Waste Management Act (Act 97 of 1980)** ([35 P.S. §§ 6018.101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=35)).<br>
Implements RCRA Subtitle D and goes beyond federal floor in some respects. Authorizes PA DEP regulation of solid waste facilities, illegal dumping enforcement (DEP enforcement; County DA prosecution authority), and municipal waste planning.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/waste/">Solid Waste, Sanitation &amp; Illegal Dumping</a>.

**Pennsylvania Hazardous Sites Cleanup Act (HSCA)** ([35 P.S. §§ 6020.101 et seq.](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=35)).<br>
State analog to CERCLA; authorizes PA DEP cleanup of hazardous-substance-release sites.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/waste/">Solid Waste, Sanitation &amp; Illegal Dumping</a>.

**PA Storm Water Management Act of 1978** ([32 P.S. §§ 680.1-680.17](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/Legis/LI/consCheck.cfm?txtType=HTM&amp;ttl=32)).<br>
Authorizes PA DEP regulation of stormwater across the Commonwealth.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/water/">Water &amp; Stormwater Infrastructure</a>.

## Philadelphia ordinances and Home Rule Charter

**Philadelphia Home Rule Charter** ([phila.gov / Home Rule Charter](https://www.phila.gov/documents/home-rule-charter/)).<br>
Establishes Philadelphia's municipal government structure under PA Home Rule. Authorizes PWD as municipal enterprise utility (rate-setting under City Council oversight); authorizes Streets Department, Sanitation Department (post-December 2025 reorganization), and Office of Clean and Green Initiatives operations. Constrains city authority to raise local revenue (Article IX of PA Constitution).<br>
*Cited in:* every D13 sub-domain.

**Philadelphia Code Title 5 (Parks and Recreation)** ([Phila. Code Title 5](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/philadelphia/latest/philadelphia_pa/0-0-0-1)).<br>
Phila Code § 5-500 governs Fairmount Park road and drive management (Streets Department jurisdiction); § 5-601 establishes the Commission on Parks and Recreation as advisory (post-2010 merger of Fairmount Park into PPR).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a>.

**Philadelphia Code Title 9 (Transportation)**.<br>
Governs Streets Department operations, traffic engineering, and public-right-of-way infrastructure.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/roads/">Roads, Bridges &amp; Pedestrian Infrastructure</a>.

**Philadelphia Code Title 10 (Regulation of Individual Conduct and Activity) and Title 21 (Public Places)** ([Phila. Code Title 10](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/philadelphia/latest/philadelphia_pa/0-0-0-1)).<br>
Includes sanitation and trash provisions: residential refuse setout, commercial waste, illegal dumping penalties, recycling participation.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/waste/">Solid Waste, Sanitation &amp; Illegal Dumping</a>.

**Philadelphia Code Title 11 (Education)**.<br>
Contains municipal education-related provisions; primary school-funding mechanism in Philadelphia is property tax with City portion supporting SDP through City-District funding agreements (cross-reference D9 SD2).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/schools/">School Buildings &amp; Public Facility Capital</a>.

**Philadelphia Code Title 15 (City-Owned Property — Recreation)**.<br>
Municipal code provisions governing PPR operations, recreation programming, facility access, and partnership architecture.<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/parks/">Parks, Recreation Centers &amp; Open Space</a>.

**Philadelphia 1% local Sales and Use Tax allocation to SEPTA under Act 84 of 2007**.<br>
Local revenue mechanism through which Philadelphia funds SEPTA operations. **MC-15** PA SUT 2% Philadelphia local allocation precision held open pending institutional retrieval (cross-reference D9 SD7).<br>
*Cited in:* <a href="/paul/campaign/empower/domain/physical-infrastructure/sub-domains/transit/">Transit &amp; Mobility</a>.
