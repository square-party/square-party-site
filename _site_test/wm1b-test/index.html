The federal-state anti-poverty architecture delivers $6,780 to this household. Federal EITC contributes $6,164 — the largest single transfer in the federal-state-local stack for a worker at $28,000 with two qualifying children. Pennsylvania's first state EITC, the Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit enacted in November 2025 at 10% of the federal credit, adds another $616. Both credits arrive automatically through tax filing. The federal anti-poverty architecture is, in this case, doing what its statutory design intends.

Of $1,047 in Philadelphia wage tax extracted from this household's $28,000 in earnings, $627 stays unrecovered. The wage tax has an income-based refund mechanism — Phila. Code § 19-1500 — that would recover $627 of the $1,047. The mechanism is gate-kept by Pennsylvania's Schedule SP eligibility, requiring a separate state approval that 95% of eligible Philadelphia filers don't navigate. The federal EITC and state WPTC both arrive automatically through tax filing. The Schedule SP refund does not. Federal, state, and local credit and tax systems run on independent administrative rails; no mechanism reconciles them at the household level. The architecture is not integrated: the federal anti-poverty credit cannot offset the local extraction.

How both sides interact summary

The architectural math for this household:

Federal-state anti-poverty architecture delivers: $6,780 ($6,164 federal EITC + $616 state WPTC, both automatic via tax filing)

Philadelphia wage tax extracts: $1,047 ($420 recoverable via Schedule SP if navigated; $627 stays unrecovered in 95% of cases)

Net experience for this household: $5,733 (typical case, Schedule SP not claimed)

The federal-state architecture is doing what its design intends; the local extraction is doing what its design intends; the architectural failure is that the two don't reconcile. Both findings are true at once.

How to claim what's owed to this household action

Concrete steps:

  1. File a federal tax return — even if income is below the filing threshold, federal EITC requires filing. Free options: IRS Free File (income under $79,000), VITA in-person assistance.
  2. Find a VITA site near Strawberry Mansion — VITA preparers are trained to claim EITC and don't charge fees. Nearest sites are typically open January through April. Find a site →
  3. Confirm WPTC is claimed automatically — when the federal return is filed, the PA-40 state return picks up WPTC eligibility. No separate application.
  4. Apply for Schedule SP wage-tax refund — separate from the federal and state filing. Requires PA Department of Revenue Schedule SP determination of low-income eligibility. Phila wage tax refund info →

Where this gap could close reform

Reform pathways currently in development:

Schedule SP automation (Philadelphia, City Council): Cross-agency automation of Schedule SP eligibility — pulling the SP determination from PA filing data rather than requiring a separate state approval — is within the City Council's existing legal authority under the Local Tax Enabling Act. Councilmembers Brooks and O'Rourke have proposed this as part of the People's Tax Plan. Read the proposal →

VITA expansion (federal-state-local): The administrative-access failure here is partly a VITA capacity gap. Expanded VITA funding, language access, and outreach would close measurable fractions of the $627-per-household unrecovered amount. Federal VITA appropriations are currently a federal matter; state and city co-investment is within Pennsylvania and Philadelphia's existing authority.

Federal-state-local credit integration: A statutory mechanism reconciling federal EITC, state WPTC, and Philadelphia wage tax at the household level would close the architectural gap. No such proposal is currently in development; this is structural reform territory.
