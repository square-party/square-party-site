The Gaps — Physical Infrastructure

A "gap" in this analysis is the distance between the formal legal architecture of an infrastructure program and the actual outcome it produces for PA-3 households. Each sub-domain has its own gap analysis, drawn from documented design features applied to documented PA-3 conditions. The patterns recur across sub-domains. This page synthesizes the recurring patterns and the documented gaps that follow from them.

The recurring patterns

Four patterns appear across the seven sub-domains.

Pattern 1 — Federal-floor enforcement without federal compliance financing. The most distinctive federal-floor finding in the domain is the AHERA enforcement / federal-funding-silence intersection at the School District of Philadelphia. The U.S. Attorney's June 2025 deferred prosecution agreement operates as criminal-floor enforcement; SDP's environmental management budget grew from $10.2 million in FY21 to $55.7 million in FY25 to meet enforcement, financed entirely from district resources and a $100 million Penn donation pledged in 2020. There is no federal program financing AHERA compliance. The pattern extends across the domain: SDWA requires drinking water quality but offers no federal water-affordability program (Philadelphia's TAP is self-financed within the PWD ratepayer base; LIHWAP operated only during the COVID period without permanent authorization). ADA Title II requires accessible sidewalks, curb ramps, pedestrian signals, and transit stops — but federal-aid project requirements attach to projects with federal funding, and Philadelphia's pre-1940 housing stock concentration corresponds to older sidewalk infrastructure with incremental retrofitting rather than systematic compliance. LCRR/LCRI requires lead service line replacement, but the customer-side line is property-owner-owned and a full-system replacement is estimated at $500 million — federal SRF financing through PENNVEST flows toward public-side work but customer-side requires homeowner participation and financing, with rental properties facing systemic delay because absentee landlords have weaker incentive to replace. Federal-criminal-floor enforcement, federal-statutory-floor obligation, federal-administrative-floor compliance — three flavors of federal floor, none with a federal financing program scaled to the obligation.

Pattern 2 — Cumulative racial disadvantage geography expressed across infrastructure systems. The same underlying demographic geography of PA-3 (established in the Identity & Legal Status analysis) expresses itself in differential infrastructure burden across five of the seven sub-domains. Lead service line concentration runs through pre-1986 rowhomes in low-income communities of color in North and West Philadelphia. The High Injury Network puts 137 of approximately 198 HIN miles in tracts scoring highest on the PDPH Underserved Communities metric, with killed-or-seriously-injured rates 2.4 times higher in those tracts. Illegal dumping concentrates in poorer neighborhoods with higher proportions of Black and Latino residents per Billy Penn's 2022 documentation. The 85 SDP buildings rated poor or unsatisfactory concentrate in older buildings disproportionately located in the Cores; the AHERA findings touch 31 schools across 2015–2023, with 7 schools named in the criminal information. Tree canopy ranges from under 5% to over 45%; the Philly Tree Plan documents a 22-degree heat differential between hottest and coolest places in the city. These are not five independent patterns. They are the same demographic geography expressing itself through five different infrastructure systems — a structural finding whose force is cumulative across sub-domains rather than additive within each.

Pattern 3 — Layered enforcement architecture with channel-specific administrative vulnerability. Federal authorization establishes the program horizon (formula apportionments, statutory floors, competitive criteria); federal appropriations populate that horizon; state implementation administers federal flows where delegated; local operations execute infrastructure delivery. The architecture's vulnerability operates at every layer. Formula programs (FTA Section 5307, FHWA NHPP/STBG/HSIP, CDBG entitlement, LWCF state-side) operate as ROBUST high-stability statutory entitlements. Competitive programs (Section 5309 CIG, IIJA-specific programs like MEGA/INFRA/BRIC, ORLP, EPA Brownfields competitive grants) operate with significant administrative discretion at every stage of project advancement. Appropriations-dependent programs face annual budget risk. The post-2025 retraction of EO 14008 (Justice40), EO 12898, and EO 14096, with all ten EPA regional environmental justice offices closed and EJScreen public access restricted, has thinned the federal administrative architecture for need-based equity targeting without changing the underlying Title VI statutory floor. The structural result: programs nominally in stable categories can experience mid-cycle administrative changes that reshape their effective scope without statutory action. The PA Act 89 transition's 2022 movement of $400 million of dedicated PA Turnpike transit funding into the state General Fund is the analogous state-level vulnerability — converting a dedicated revenue stream into an annual budget negotiation, and producing the structural state-match vulnerability that drove SEPTA's 2024–2026 fiscal crisis.

Pattern 4 — Mid-cycle program-horizon vulnerability. Even within the stable formula-program architecture, mid-cycle action can reshape the program horizon. P.L. 119-75 (Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, signed February 3, 2026) restructured approximately $2.3 billion in IIJA-tied funds via transfers and rescissions, including approximately $879 million from NEVI alone. OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025) rescinded unobligated funds across multiple IRA programs (Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund statutory authority repealed; Environmental Justice Block Grants eliminated; Climate Pollution Reduction Grants eliminated; Neighborhood Access and Equity rescinded; IRA sustainable forestry funds rescinded — relevant to the parks sub-domain's canopy work; HUD Green and Resilient Retrofit rescinded). The Land and Water Conservation Fund's permanent $900 million annual appropriation under the Great American Outdoors Act was protected by Congress's Interior and Environment Appropriations Subcommittee against the FY 2026 administration proposal of a 43% diversion — but the proposal demonstrates that mid-cycle attempts at administrative diversion of even mandatory-funded programs are within the operating universe. The structural finding: federal program horizons are not stable across the multi-year cycle even when the legal architecture appears to provide stability. Treating reauthorization-period announcements as the beginning of representational work rather than its conclusion is the operational implication.

Gaps by sub-domain

Each sub-domain's full gap analysis lives on its own page; brief summaries below.

Transit & Mobility — Title VI fare and service equity adequacy for the June 2025 SEPTA action; ADA Title II 46% station accessibility deficit; PA Act 89 transit funding state-match structural vulnerability; cross-jurisdictional commute pattern federal-program engagement; federal operating-support capacity below operational need; SEPTA CIG project advancement uncertainty (KOP Rail / Trolley Modernization).

Water & Stormwater — Customer-side lead service line replacement financing structural gap; federal water-affordability program absence; tenure-based lead service line replacement delay; Green City, Clean Waters compliance pace mismatch; Eastwick flood resilience long-term funding uncertainty; TAP enrollment-vs-eligibility participation gap; Title VI environmental justice review of LSL replacement prioritization.

Roads, Bridges & Pedestrian Infrastructure — Title VI cumulative-impact analysis for HIN-UC concentration; ADA Title II sidewalk and curb ramp compliance pace; local-street capital federal funding structural limit; Vision Zero state-legislation needs; bridge inventory PA-3-specific data; HIN federal funding pipeline post-IIJA.

Solid Waste & Sanitation — Enforcement-scale gap relative to dumping volume (~31 max annual arrests vs ~6,800–11,500 tons/year dumping volume; 2-staff PPD Environmental Crimes Unit); federal anti-dumping program absence; vacant lot remediation scale relative to dumping geography; twice-weekly pickup expansion criteria transparency; recycling participation data by neighborhood.

School Buildings — AHERA federal compliance financing absence (the most distinctive federal-floor gap in the entire domain); William Penn $3.8B remaining adequacy gap notwithstanding partial legislative response; PlanCon 2.0 unfunded since 2019; Title VI cumulative-impact analysis for building condition concentration; anchor institution / SDP environmental management support transparency; federal IIJA cross-cutting school capital programs limited and OBBBA-narrowed program horizon.

Parks & Open Space — Park-quality equity gaps despite high-access metric (TPL Equity 72/100; 14% / 36% park-space gaps for low-income vs average vs high-income neighborhoods); tree canopy and heat-vulnerability differentials (5% vs 45% canopy; 22-degree heat differential); 73,860 residents without nearby park access; LWCF/ORLP equity-targeting structural limitation under thinned federal EJ architecture; federal IRA Forest Service urban canopy program implementation narrowed under OBBBA; pool operations and seasonal staffing capacity.

Federal Infrastructure Funding — IIJA reauthorization status with no bill yet introduced (most consequential near-term federal lever for the entire domain); Justice40 framework revocation and equity-targeting effects; mid-cycle rescission risk and OBBBA-attributable program-horizon narrowing; federal-criminal-floor enforcement without federal compliance financing (the cross-domain pattern); authorization-to-disbursement gap as structural feature; cross-jurisdictional coordination requirements (Eastwick / Cobbs Creek as model); PA-3-specific federal funding aggregate data gap.
