Recent Changes — Physical Infrastructure

The 2024–2026 period has been one of structural disruption across the physical-infrastructure domain. Federal: IIJA approaches expiration with no reauthorization introduced; OBBBA was enacted July 2025 with significant IRA-program rescissions; Justice40 was revoked; P.L. 119-75 transferred or rescinded approximately $2.3 billion in IIJA funds in February 2026; LWCF was protected by Congress against an administration cut proposal. State: SEPTA's fiscal crisis produced a court order, a capital-to-operating bridge, and continued reliance on the same mechanism in FY27; Pennsylvania's $565 million November 2025 William Penn response runs against an estimated ~$3.8 billion remaining adequacy gap. Local: Sanitation/Streets split implemented; Vision Zero Action Plan 2030 released; AHERA DPA operational under judicial oversight; SDP "Accelerting Opportunity" plan at pending vote; FDR Park advancing past $111 million in invested funds. Each entry below documents a change material to the analysis with cross-references to the affected sub-domains.

April 30, 2026 (pending)

SDP "Accelerating Opportunity" Facilities Master Plan vote (postponed from April 23)

The School District of Philadelphia's 10-year Facilities Master Plan, "Accelerating Opportunity," is on the Board of Education agenda. Initial recommendations were released January 22, 2026; Superintendent Tony B. Watlington Sr. presented the first version to the Board on February 26, 2026; the revised final version was presented April 20, 2026. Final scope: 17 closures (revised down from 20), 169 modernizations (revised up from 159), 6 co-locations (maintained), $3 billion total cost (revised up from $2.8 billion). The District plans to put $1 billion toward the upgrades and is requesting $2 billion from state and philanthropic sources. The Board vote was originally scheduled for April 23, 2026 but was delayed to April 30, 2026 "in the spirit of partnership with the communities we serve" (per Board President Streater) at City Council request. City Council requested further postponement to May 28, 2026 at the earliest. Mayor Cherelle Parker supports the plan and is pushing a $1-per-trip rideshare tax to help fund the SDP $300 million budget shortfall.

Affects: School Buildings. Vote outcome remains an open verification item.

February 19, 2026

Shapiro FY 2026–27 budget proposal with dedicated transit funding

Governor Shapiro's FY 2026–27 budget proposal includes transferring an additional 1.75% of Pennsylvania Sales and Use Tax revenues to the Pennsylvania Transportation Trust Fund beginning July 1, 2027 — providing approximately $300–319.6 million annually in recurring transit funding statewide. Also proposes an additional $565 million for school districts with adequacy gap. The FY 2026–27 budget has not been enacted as of April 27, 2026; the deadline is June 30, 2026. The Republican-controlled state Senate has previously declined to advance Shapiro's recurring-transit-funding proposal; the structural impasse on long-term recurring transit funding remains unresolved despite the cumulative emergency interventions documented through 2025.

Affects: Transit & Mobility; School Buildings; Federal Infrastructure Funding.

February 3, 2026

P.L. 119-75 — IIJA transfers and rescissions (~$2.3 billion)

The Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75 / H.R. 7148) was signed February 3, 2026, ending a partial federal government shutdown. Per CRS R48845 and R48728, the THUD Act provisions transferred approximately $2.3 billion in unobligated IIJA balances rather than purely rescinding them — moving funds from the NEVI Program (~$879 million: $503.8M formula + $300M competitive + $75M Joint Office) and the Reduction of Truck Emissions at Port Facilities Program into the Tribal Transportation Program, the INFRA Program, and the Reconnecting Communities Program. The Reconnecting Communities Program was funded at $30 million — 15% of the $200 million obligated for FY2026 in the original IIJA. CRISI rail program received only $7 million in new appropriations in FY26 (95% of its $137M total derived from IIJA unobligated balances; 93% reduction from FY2025).

Affects: Transit & Mobility; Roads, Bridges & Pedestrian; Federal Infrastructure Funding. Demonstrates that mid-cycle transfer/rescission risk applies to nominally stable program categories.

December 17, 2025

PWD lead service line inventory update and 2026 pilot replacement program

Per WHYY reporting and PWD: of Philadelphia's 511,000 total service lines, 16,805 confirmed to contain lead, 157,823 confirmed lead-free, 351,514 of unknown material. Approximately 1 in 20 (5%) of properties may have a lead service line. PWD has determined the materials of approximately 85,000 previously-unknown lines since October 2024 (when PWD launched its public Service Line Materials Map). Approximately 480,000 letters covering approximately 360,000 properties were sent in December 2025. The Service Line Verification Program limited targeted excavations to the first 400 customers responding spring/early summer 2025. The 2026 pilot Service Line Replacement Program is the first time PWD will replace lines outside of planned water main construction or HELP loan requests; approximately 1,000 lead service lines are budgeted for replacement in North and West Philadelphia, beginning 2026. Total estimated cost of replacing all known lead service lines is approximately $500 million.

Affects: Water & Stormwater.

November 25, 2025

Vision Zero Action Plan 2030 released

Released by OTIS, Vision Zero Philadelphia, the Department of Public Health (PDPH), the Department of Streets, and the Office of Emergency Management, fulfilling Mayor Parker's March 2024 executive order. The new High Injury Network is based on 2019–2023 crash data and reaffirms that 12% of streets account for 80% of fatal/serious injury crashes. PDPH developed an Underserved Communities (UC) metric — KSI crash rate 2.4 times higher in highest-UC tracts vs. lowest-UC tracts. Approximately one-third (137 miles) of the HIN is located in highest-UC tracts; only 14% (61 miles) in lowest-UC tracts. The Plan commits to safety upgrades on every mile of the HIN by 2030. Pilot of Intelligent Speed Assistance (ISA) in municipal fleet. Five neighborhood slow zones (20 mph limits) completed; concrete pedestrian median islands installed on Race, Chestnut, and Broad Streets.

Affects: Roads, Bridges & Pedestrian Infrastructure. Confirms and substantially strengthens the structural finding that fatal/serious injury crashes are concentrated in lower-income and disproportionately minority areas.

November 12 + 24, 2025

PA FY 2025–26 budget enacted (Day 135 of impasse) and follow-on $219.9M for SEPTA Silverliner IV

The Pennsylvania state FY 2025–26 budget was enacted on November 12, 2025 — Day 135 of an unprecedented budget impasse, the longest in state history. The enacted budget allocated $565 million in adequacy-related school funding: approximately $526.4 million through the bipartisan adequacy formula, $32.2 million in tax equity supplement, and $6.4 million through a minimum-baseline provision guaranteeing each of approximately 500 districts at least $50,000 in additional funding. The William Penn School District received approximately $80 million total. The budget also included $665 million for total public schools, $175 million in cyber charter reform, and $125 million for school facilities — though the facility allocation is not formally tied to the William Penn formula. No new transit funding was included; Democrats removed transit from negotiations after Senate Republicans refused to advance long-term funding. On November 24, 2025, Shapiro directed PennDOT Secretary Mike Carroll to allocate $219.9 million in additional capital funding to SEPTA for Silverliner IV safety upgrades, restoration of full Regional Rail service, and federal safety order compliance.

Affects: School Buildings; Transit & Mobility.

August 24 – September 14, 2025

SEPTA fiscal crisis sequence — service cuts, court reversal, capital-to-operating buffer

The SEPTA board's June 26, 2025 service-cut authorization was implemented in stages, then partially reversed and structurally averted:

  • August 24: first wave — 32 bus routes eliminated, 16 shortened, broader frequency reductions, all special services ended.
  • September 1: 21.5% fare increase took effect; $2.90 base fare for bus and Metro (tying NYC MTA for highest in the country).
  • September 4: Judge Sierra Thomas-Street, Philadelphia Court of Common Pleas, ordered SEPTA to immediately reverse all service cuts (32 routes restored, 16 extensions restored, no further reductions permitted "until further order of court"). Order did not prohibit fare increases. Plaintiffs: Lance Haver and two riders, represented by attorney George Bochetto. Lawsuit theory: cuts disproportionately burdened Black, Latino, and low-income riders while sparing Regional Rail predominantly serving wealthier suburban commuters.
  • September 8: Shapiro Administration approved SEPTA's request to use up to $394 million of its FY 2025–26 capital assistance allocation that had yet to be obligated for operating purposes (this is SEPTA's own unobligated capital, not a redirection of PennDOT or PTTF dollars).
  • September 14: full service restored; the 21.5% fare increase took effect simultaneously.
  • October 8: Bochetto filed proposed class action targeting fare increases.
  • October 2025: Federal Railroad Administration ordered emergency safety inspections of SEPTA's 223 Silverliner IV cars after a series of electrical fires; major Regional Rail service disruptions through fall.
  • January 1, 2026: the second-wave five-Regional-Rail-line elimination (Paoli/Thorndale, Trenton, Wilmington/Newark, Chestnut Hill West, Cynwyd), 18 additional bus routes, and 9 PM curfew did not occur, following the September 4 court order combined with the $394M capital-to-operating buffer.

Affects: Transit & Mobility. The crisis is an artifact of Pennsylvania's Act 89 transit-funding architecture, not of national-level transit-funding decline.

July 4, 2025

OBBBA enacted — significant IRA program rescissions narrow federal infrastructure horizon

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21) was signed July 4, 2025, restructuring the federal infrastructure-program horizon between IIJA passage and IIJA expiration. Key infrastructure-relevant provisions: $12.5 billion for FAA air traffic control modernization; significant rescissions of unobligated IIJA/IRA funds (Low-Carbon Transportation Materials Program $1.9B unobligated rescinded; Clean Heavy-Duty Vehicles ~$454M unobligated rescinded; Greenhouse Gas Reduction Fund statutory authority repealed; Environmental Justice Block Grants eliminated; Climate Pollution Reduction Grants eliminated; Neighborhood Access and Equity Grant Program rescinded — relevant because this program was the primary IRA-era complement to IIJA Reconnecting Communities for safety and access projects; state and local environmental review capacity-building IRA $100M repealed); EV tax credits phased out by September 2025; EV charging infrastructure tax credits phased out by mid-2026; methane emissions fee repealed for 10-year period; 125% optional fee for expedited NEPA environmental review; renewable energy on public lands new 3.9% capacity fee; municipal bond tax-exempt status preserved (significant positive for cities); Low-Income Housing Tax Credit allocations expanded by 12% for 9% credits. OBBBA also rescinded unobligated funds for IRA sustainable forestry programs (relevant to urban canopy work in the parks sub-domain), HUD Green and Resilient Retrofit Program, and various NOAA coastal-resilience programs.

Affects: Parks & Open Space; School Buildings; Federal Infrastructure Funding.

June 26, 2025

USAO-EDPA AHERA criminal information and DPA against the School District of Philadelphia

The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania filed criminal charges and a deferred prosecution agreement against the School District of Philadelphia. U.S. Attorney David Metcalf announced the charges and DPA. This was the first time in U.S. history that a school district was criminally charged with this type of environmental violation, and the first use of a DPA in a case against a public school district in an AHERA case. The charges resulted from a five-year investigation conducted by USAO-EDPA, the DOJ Environmental Crimes Section, and EPA-CID. The DPA includes 8 criminal counts between June 2018 and April 2023 involving failures to conduct mandatory three-year inspections at at least seven schools (S. Weir Mitchell Elementary, Charles W. Henry Elementary, and others), plus failure to conduct a timely six-month inspection at one school (Building 21 Alternative High School); 61 findings citing problems in 31 schools from 2015 to 2023; a 5-year monitoring period; regular compliance reports to the federal court for five years. As of Grid Magazine reporting in January 2026, the DPA is operationally in effect, with the District under judicial oversight. SDP's environmental management budget grew from $10.2 million in FY21 to $55.7 million in FY25 to meet enforcement; eighteen new positions were added; SDP entered a $24.2 million multi-year contract with Tetra Tech for inspections.

Affects: School Buildings. The most distinctive federal-floor finding in the entire physical-infrastructure domain — federal-criminal-floor enforcement operating without federal compliance financing.

May 17, 2024

Sanitation/Streets reorganization and Office of Clean and Green Initiatives

Mayor Cherelle Parker's executive order and memorandum of understanding (signed May 17, 2024) split Sanitation from the Streets Department, creating a stand-alone Department of Sanitation under Commissioner Crystal Jacobs Shipman; the Streets Department continues under Commissioner Kristin Del Rossi; the Office of Clean and Green Initiatives (former Streets Commissioner Carlton Williams as Director) coordinates the citywide cleanliness mission. The 38-member Clean and Green Cabinet was formally established by executive order in May 2024. The City's FY25 budget increased the Taking Care of Business Clean Corridors Program from 49 to 129 corridors and funded an additional 1,500 Bigbelly trash cans. The One Philly, A United City Illegal Dumping Task Force was announced October 2025 and launched November 3, 2025; SWEEP officers and Park Rangers received NOV training.

Affects: Solid Waste & Sanitation; Parks & Open Space (Park Rangers received NOV training).

January – February 2025

Justice40 framework revoked; equity-targeting administrative architecture thinned

EO 14008 (which established Justice40 in January 2021), EO 12898 (Clinton 1994, the foundational federal environmental justice mandate), and EO 14096 (Biden 2023, "Revitalizing Our Nation's Commitment to Environmental Justice for All") were all revoked or rescinded between January and February 2025. Per GAO and Sustainability Directory reporting, all 10 EPA regional environmental justice offices were closed; EPA's EJScreen tool was prohibited from use in enforcement and compliance activities; $1 billion in IRA EJ community grants was targeted. Justice40 had covered 518 programs across 16 federal agencies with approximately $613 billion in funding. Per Urban Institute November 2025 retrospective: DOE and EPA increased disadvantaged-county funding faster than overall growth during the Justice40 period; DOT and DOI lagged. Post-revocation distributional patterns are a structural unknown affecting all federal infrastructure flows to PA-3.

Affects: Water & Stormwater; Roads, Bridges & Pedestrian; Parks & Open Space; Federal Infrastructure Funding. The substantive Title VI statutory floor remains operative, but the federal administrative architecture for need-based equity targeting has been significantly thinned.
