# Temporal Changes Sidecar — D7 Land & Property — 2026-05-03

**Domain:** D7 Land & Property
**Cycle:** M2 v1.2 first verified-cycle Phase 3
**Verification date:** 2026-05-03
**Format:** M0 v2.1 Standard 18.A vector format — anchor + dated vector points + analytical consequence
**Purpose:** Cumulative cross-cycle persistence record for D7 temporal-change tracking. Subsequent verification cycles append new dated points to existing vectors rather than overwriting. The sidecar is the structurally-distinct artifact from the Material Changes Summary at the front of the verified file (which uses Standard 11 four-field within-document navigation format); both are written in the same coordinated Phase 3 verification pass per M2 v1.2 §7 Capability 2.

**Vector-construction discipline.** Each TC-NN entry below is an anchored vector — the anchor names the architectural element being tracked (statute; rule; agency; appropriation; program); the dated points name observed states at specific dates; the analytical consequence names the SD-level findings the vector calibrates. Phase 3 entries operate as Phase 1 starting points where empirical retrieval was feasible at web-search level; subsequent-cycle accumulation is the mechanism by which the held-open displacement-magnitude question (G7-SD1-03) and other Track 2 questions become empirically tractable through cross-cycle vector accumulation rather than single-cycle assertion.

## TC-01: HUD AFFH rule operative-content vector (anchored to G7-SD4-01; T7-SD4-03 at draft date)

**Anchor:** Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing rule (24 CFR Part 5; Subpart A) operationalizing FHA § 3608 statutory mandate; HUD program-participant obligation to AFFH in connection with Consolidated Plans, Annual Action Plans, and PHA Plans.

**Dated vector points:**
- **2015-07-16:** 2015 AFFH Final Rule effective (Obama administration). Substantive: requires Assessment of Fair Housing (AFH) with HUD-provided data tools, fair-housing-issue analysis, and goals incorporated in Consolidated Plans.
- **2018-01:** HUD suspends AFH submission requirements pending replacement (Carson HUD).
- **2020-09-08:** Preserving Community and Neighborhood Choice rule effective (first Trump administration). Replaces AFH with simplified self-certification; substantive analytical-and-planning obligation eliminated.
- **2021-06-10:** Restoring Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing Definitions and Certifications IFR effective (Biden administration). Reinstates 2015 AFFH definitions; voluntary fair-housing planning process; AFH not required.
- **2023-02:** Notice of Proposed Rulemaking proposing return to substantive AFH obligation via "equity plan" framing.
- **2025-01-16:** HUD withdraws 2023 NPRM (per NACo coverage of January 16, 2025 Federal Register withdrawal).
- **2025-03-05 (approximate):** HUD issues Interim Final Rule "Restoring Affirmatively Furthering Fair Housing Definitions and Certifications" (second Trump administration). IFR retains AFFH definition and certification but removes substantive AFH analytical-and-planning obligation; self-attestation framework.
- **2026-04-06:** HUD withdraws eight FHEO guidance documents in Federal Register notice (per NLIHC April 13, 2026 update).
- **2026-05-03:** Status at Phase 3 verification — IFR remains pending finalization; no successor "equity plan" rulemaking anticipated at HUD.

**Analytical consequence (D7-internal).** The vector documents the structural-vulnerability framing the substructure flagged: the rule's substantive content cycles across administrations within a stable statutory-mandate floor. G7-SD4-01 (AFFH operationalization gap) is calibrated against this vector — each subsequent verification cycle appends the operative state at that date; the analytical finding "framework above the statutory floor is administratively vulnerable" is operationalized empirically by the vector's documented multiplicity of states.

**Subsequent-cycle accumulation note.** Future D7 reverification cycles append the operative IFR / final-rule / replacement state at the verification date to this vector. The vector is the cross-cycle persistence record; the Material Changes Summary at the front of any single verified file documents only the within-cycle change.

## TC-02: HUD disparate-impact rule operative-content vector (anchored to G7-SD4-02; T7-SD4-01 at draft date)

**Anchor:** HUD's Implementation of the Fair Housing Act's Disparate Impact Standard, codified at 24 CFR Part 100 Subpart G § 100.500; operationalizes the *Texas Dep't of Housing & Cmty. Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project*, 576 U.S. 519 (2015) burden-shifting framework.

**Dated vector points:**
- **2013-02-15:** 2013 final rule effective; codifies disparate-impact theory + burden-shifting test (78 Fed. Reg. 11460).
- **2015-06-25:** *Inclusive Communities* Supreme Court decision recognizes disparate-impact claims under FHA.
- **2020-09-24:** 2020 rule effective (first Trump administration); heightens pleading requirements; revises burden-shifting; 84 Fed. Reg. 42854. Federal court preliminary injunction (D. Mass.) prevents 2020 rule taking effect.
- **2023-03-31:** 2023 reinstatement rule effective (Biden administration); restores 2013 framework; 88 Fed. Reg. 19450.
- **2025-04-23:** Executive Order 14281 ("Restoring Equality of Opportunity and Meritocracy") directs federal agencies to review and revise/rescind disparate-impact-imposing regulations.
- **2025-08-04 (approximate):** HUD submits draft final rule to OMB for review under EO 12866.
- **2025-12-09:** DOJ publishes final rule rescinding disparate-impact provisions in Title VI implementing regulations.
- **2026-01-14:** HUD publishes NPRM (91 Fed. Reg. 1475) proposing to remove and reserve 24 CFR Part 100 Subpart G entirely without replacement; 30-day comment period.
- **2026-02-13:** Comment period closes (1,109 comments received per regulations.gov).
- **2026-05-03:** Status at Phase 3 verification — final rule pending; *Inclusive Communities* binding precedent unaffected; state fair-housing laws (PHRA) authorizing disparate-impact unaffected.

**Analytical consequence (D7-internal).** G7-SD4-02 (disparate-impact framework operationalization gap) calibrated against this vector. The structural finding "regulatory-architecture-above-statutory-floor administratively vulnerable" is reinforced by the cycle pattern (2013 → 2020 enjoined → 2023 → 2026 proposed rescission). The disconnect between regulatory cycles and binding precedent is itself analytically significant: rescission of the regulatory burden-shifting framework does not extinguish the cause of action.

**Subsequent-cycle accumulation note.** Future cycles append the operative rule status at verification date.

## TC-06: Philadelphia Right to Counsel coverage vector (anchored to G7-SD3-05; T7-SD3-04 at draft date)

- **2019-11:** Ordinance passed by City Council establishing RTC.
- **2021-12:** Implementing regulation enacted.
- **2022-02-01:** Phase 1 — initial rollout in ZIP codes 19139 and 19121 (West and North Philadelphia).
- **2023-02-01:** Phase 2 — expansion to 19134 and 19144 (Port Richmond / Kensington / Germantown / portions Mt. Airy / East Falls).
- **2025-01-21 (approximate):** Phase 3 — expansion to 19124, 19141, 19154 (8 ZIP codes total).
- **2026-04-28:** Phase 4 — expansion to 19131, 19153 (10 ZIP codes total). RTC now covers more than 35% of Philadelphia renters facing eviction.
- **2026-05-03:** Status at Phase 3 verification — 10 ZIP codes covered; phased citywide rollout continues.

**Outcome data (FY25, per April 28, 2026 press release):**
- Households served by PEPP partners: 4,600+ (1,750+ in RTC ZIPs)
- Families served: ~50% (1,793 children directly impacted)
- Tenants with disability: ~50%
- Older adults: 20%+
- Default judgments for PEPP-represented: 0% (vs. 35-40% unrepresented)
- Twice-as-likely to prevail at trial when represented
- Housing preserved/stabilized/improved: 80%+
- Critical financial relief for remaining cases: 70%
- Eviction filings reduced citywide ~40% via RTC + PEPP + EDP combined.

**Analytical consequence (D7-internal).** G7-SD3-05 (RTC implementation gap; representation-architecture gap) substantively reinforced and refined.
